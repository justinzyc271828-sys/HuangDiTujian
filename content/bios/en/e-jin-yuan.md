# Emperor Yuan of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Rui, styled Jingwen, was the son of Sima Jin, Prince Gong of Langya, and a nephew of Emperor Wu. In the War of the Eight Princes he was sent to garrison Xiapi, and in the 1st year of Yongjia, on Wang Dao's counsel, crossed south to govern Jianye with command of Yangzhou's armies. The southern houses would not at first submit; but Wang Dao and his brother Dun pushed him forward with all their hearts, and the notables of the three Wu commanderies came over — the arrangement of "Wang and Sima sharing the realm" began here.

In the 1st year of Jianwu, with Emperor Min's fate unconfirmed, Rui styled himself King of Jin; in the 1st year of Taixing, on word of Min's death, he took the throne with his capital at Jiankang. He gathered officers widely, most of them the leading men of the south, and sent Zu Ti north — striking the oars midstream, Zu recovered the lands south of the Yellow River. But Yuan's "frugality and reverence were more than sufficient, his heroic measure wanting": he let Wang Dun command Jingzhou and the surrounding provinces with the armies outside the capital, then raised Liu Kui and Diao Xie against the Wangs — and provoked Dun's revolt. In the 1st year of Yongchang Dun rose from Wuchang, forced his way into Jiankang, slew Diao Xie and others, and the emperor, falling ill of grief and rage, died in the intercalary month, aged forty-seven.

A distant kinsman of the imperial house, Yuan crossed south and saved the caps and gowns, re-founding the Jin line — a service to the altars. But the co-rule of the crossed clans and the local houses pushed the throne aside and the great clans up; the age of aristocratic politics, and of "Wang and Sima sharing the realm," had begun.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 6 ("Benji of Emperor Yuan"), ch. 65 (biography of Wang Dao).

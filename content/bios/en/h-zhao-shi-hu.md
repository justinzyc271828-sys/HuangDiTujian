# Shi Hu, Martial Emperor of Later Zhao · Principal Deeds

Shi Hu, styled Jilong, was the nephew of Shi Le, a fierce warrior who took cities for his uncle as one of his field commanders. In the 8th year of Xianhe Le died; Hu, by the late will, assisted Shi Hong — and at once seized power. In the 9th year he deposed and slew Hong and stood himself, calling himself Heavenly King of Zhao Regent, later emperor. He moved the capital to Ye, raised palaces and parks on a grand scale, and "drove over 400,000 laborers" to build the Huayuan park and hunting grounds; he swept the realm's women into his harem — the records say the people grieved and raged.

His tyranny was the worst of the Sixteen States: corvée without limit, slaughter without mercy; and with his sons contending, kin slew kin. He campaigned against Former Yan and Former Liang, his arms at their height while the people's strength ran out. In the 5th year of Yonghe he died; his sons fought for the throne, and Shi Min (Ran Min) rose in the chaos and slew over 200,000 Hu — Later Zhao collapsed at once. It rose in tyranny and fell in a moment.

The historians say of him: "With a savage nature and a brutal temper, though he put on the robes of civilization, his virtue was the wolf's." The realm's strength was spent in cruelty; and the Hu–Han slaughter of his fall was the bitterest of the barbarian age. His rise and ruin stand as the warning of tyranny.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 106 (records of Shi Jilong), ch. 107 (records of Shi Min).

# Emperor Xiaowu of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Yao, styled Changming, was the son of Emperor Jianwen by Lady Li. On Jianwen's death in the 2nd year of Xian'an he took the throne at eleven; Empress Chu held court for a time, and Xie An governed. In the 1st year of Ningkang Huan Wen died, the menace of abdication was gone, and Eastern Jin had its chance. In the 1st year of Taiyuan the emperor took the government in person.

His reign is defined by the Battle of the Fei River: in the 8th year of Taiyuan Fu Jian of Former Qin came south in overwhelming force, "a million men," and Xie Shi and Xie Xuan met him with eighty thousand of the Northern Garrison. At the Fei River the Qin army collapsed — "every grass and tree a soldier, the wind and cranes the enemy's cries" — and Fu Jian fled north in rout. Eastern Jin was saved, and the century of the southern dynasties was set. Pressing north on the victory, the Jin armies took the cities of Henan — won and lost again. In the 10th year of Taiyuan Xie An died, and the emperor turned to his brother Sima Daozi; the way of government decayed.

In person rule he sank into wine and women, drinking daily with Daozi while the government went slack; in the 21st year of Taiyuan, drunk, he mocked Lady Zhang, who in terror had her maids smother him in the Qingshu Hall — twenty-four years a sovereign, and a middling restorer killed by a jest at a woman's hands, aged thirty-five.

The historians say: "Relying on Xie An at first, the Fei River battle saved the south; later he drowned in wine and women, and left government to Daozi." The Fei River was the summit of Eastern Jin's recovery and the hinge of its decline: after it, Daozi and the Huans contended, Sun En and Lu Xun rose, and Liu Yu, a common soldier, rose from Jingkou — the fall of Eastern Jin was already in view in Xiaowu's reign.

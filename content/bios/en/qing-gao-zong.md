# Hongli, Emperor Gaozong of Qing (Qianlong) · Major Deeds

Aisin Gioro Hongli (1711-1799), fourth son of [[qing-shi-zong-y|Emperor Shizong]], took the throne in 1735, era Qianlong.

His sixty years (plus three as retired emperor) were the summit of Qing power: the Ten Complete Campaigns - the Zunghars and the Muslim cities pacified, Jinchuan, Burma, Vietnam, the Gurkhas - all by generals, never in person - brought the map to its greatest extent; the Four Treasuries gathered all letters, though with censorship built in and many books destroyed. Six southern tours and lavish circuits drained the treasury; in his old age Heshen rose and officialdom rotted.

In 1796 he abdicated to [[qing-ren-zong|Renzong]] and reigned on as retired emperor, dying in 1799. The zenith of the splendor was also the seedbed of decline - the opium habit was already planted.

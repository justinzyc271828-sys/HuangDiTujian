# Zhao Ji, Emperor Huizong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Ji, eleventh son of [[n-song-shen|Emperor Shenzong]], ascended in 1100. He is among the greatest artists ever to sit on a Chinese throne: the slender-gold calligraphic script, bird-and-flower painting, the Xuanhe Painting Academy - his artistry crowned the age. As an emperor, however, his record is the ruin of Northern Song.

In 1102 he made [[cai-jing|Cai Jing]] chancellor, erected the stele proscribing the Yuanyou faction, and launched the Flower-and-Rock Network that stripped the southeast of its finest stones and plants, breeding seething resentment. In 1120 Fang La rose in Muzhou and shook the southeast. Abroad he allied with the Jin to destroy Liao, recovering the empty city of Yanjing in 1123 on tribute terms - in reality inviting the wolf into the house.

In 1125, with Jin armies advancing, Huizong abdicated hastily to his son Zhao Huan, [[n-song-qin|Emperor Qinzong]], and became retired emperor. In 1127 both emperors were seized and taken north; Huizong died at Wuguocheng in 1135.

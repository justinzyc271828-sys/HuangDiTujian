# Emperor Taiwu of Northern Wei · Key Events

Tuoba Tao was the third emperor of Northern Wei. In Taichang 8 (423) Emperor Mingyuan died and he ascended the throne at Pingcheng, changing the era name to Shiguang the following year; over twenty-nine years on the throne, Northern Wei entered its peak of expansion. This page takes 《魏书·世祖纪》 as the backbone of its chronology; the persecution of Buddhism is cross-referenced in 《魏书·释老志》.

His martial record is the hardest line in his annals: in Shiguang 4 (427) he captured Tongwan, capital of Hu Xia; in Shenjia 4 (431) he destroyed the Helian Xia and took Guanzhong; in Taiyan 2 (436) he destroyed the Feng family's Northern Yan and western Liaoning fell to Wei; in Taiyan 5 (439) his troops reached Guzang and destroyed the Juqu Northern Liang. With that, the north was unified under Northern Wei, and the Sixteen Kingdoms period came to an end.

Beyond the campaigns, domestic and religious policy were no less striking. In Taiping Zhenjun 6 (445) Ge Wu rose in revolt in Guanzhong and was put down by Wei forces; from Taiping Zhenjun 5 (444) the court banned Buddhism and destroyed temples, and in Zhenjun 7 (446) an edict ordered the provinces to bury monks alive—the persecution reached its peak. The record names Cui Hao and Kou Qianzhi as figures tied to his religious turn, and this remains the most contested side of his later reputation.

In Taiping Zhenjun 11 (450) he launched a massive southern campaign against Liu Song, driving straight to Guabu on the north bank of the Yangzi and facing Emperor Wen of Song across the river—an awesome display that still failed to settle the south. In Zhengping 2 (452) he was murdered at Pingcheng by the palace attendant Zong Ai, and Northern Wei politics fell into turmoil.

A thread for reading across pages: set against his successor [[n-wei-xiaowen|Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei]], one sees Northern Wei's long turn from cavalry expansion to sinicizing reform; the juxtaposition of supreme military achievement with the persecution of Buddhism and a murdered emperor's end is exactly why the appraisal of him runs neutral-to-negative.

# Emperor Xiaoming of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Xu, second son of Emperor Xuanwu, took the throne at six on his father's death in the 4th year of Yanchang, with Empress Dowager Hu holding the court in her own name. His reign was the hinge of Northern Wei's fall: in the 1st year of Zhengguang, Yuan Cha and Liu Teng confined the empress in the northern palace and ruled for years, and the government decayed; in the 4th year of Zhengguang Poliuhan Baling rose at Woye garrison — the Six Garrisons rising swept the north, and the foundations of Wei trembled.

In the 1st year of Wutai the empress dowager returned to power and had Xiaoming poisoned, aged nineteen, setting up the infant Yuan Zhao; Erzhu Rong, taking this as his cause, marched south, drowned the empress and the infant in the river, and slew over two thousand princes and ministers — the Heyin massacre. The princes of Wei were spent, and the realm was a name only.

Xiaoming, "a child in the storm, the power with his mother and the strong ministers," sat thirteen years in the grip of Empress Hu and Yuan Cha, unable to move. The Six Garrisons rising was the fuse of Wei's ruin, and his death and the Heyin massacre opened the split of the realm — the fall of Northern Wei began in his reign.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 9 ("Benji of Emperor Xiaoming"), ch. 13 (biographies of the empresses).

# Zhao Xu, Emperor Shenzong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Xu, eldest son of [[n-song-ying|Emperor Yingzong]], ascended in 1067 at twenty, determined to make the state rich and strong.

In 1069 he elevated Wang Anshi, established the Three-Service Finance Commission, and rolled out the green sprouts, hired service, militia, and equal transport laws - the Xining Reforms. They rebuilt finances and armies, but charges of burdening the people and seizing profits followed at once, and the court split into reform and conservative camps. Wang Anshi resigned for good in 1076; the reforms continued while the reform camp itself split. The Yuanfeng restructuring of offices completed the recentralization.

Militarily Shenzong dreamed of expansion: the Xining campaigns opened the Longyou lands of Western Xia, but in 1081 five columns invaded Xia and lost tens of thousands at Lingzhou and Yongle. The western war came to nothing, and the emperor mourned the Yongle disaster openly at court.

He died in 1085; his son Zhao Xu succeeded under the regency of Empress Gao, becoming [[n-song-zhe|Emperor Zhezong]].

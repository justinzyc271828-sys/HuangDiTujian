# Emperor Ping of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Kan was the son of Liu Xing, the filial king of Zhongshan, born in Zhongshan. When [[han-ai-di|Emperor Ai]] died without issue in the second Yuanshou year, the grand empress dowager Wang Zhengjun and [[xin-wang-mang|Wang Mang]] settled on him, and the nine-year-old king of Zhongshan was brought to Chang'an as Emperor Ping. From his first day "all policy came from Mang," written into every character of the last era name of Western Han.

The Yuanshi years looked like an age of flourishing culture, but every flourish was Wang Mang's theater: in the first year, the Zhao empress dowager of Cheng and the Fu empress dowager of Ai were deposed, and Mang took the title "Duke Who Settles Han"; in the second, Mang's daughter married the emperor and was made empress; in the third, ten thousand quarters were built for scholars, with state altars and schools established through every commandery and district; in the fourth Mang took the title "Chief of the Realm," above the Three Dukes, and the ministers petitioned the Nine Distinctions, which he accepted — Duke Who Settles Han, Chief of the Realm, Nine Distinctions: the ceremonial steps of usurpation completed one by one, each step adding one dry line of dating to Ping's annals.

In the twelfth month of the fifth Yuanshi year Ping died at fourteen. The benji records only the word "died"; the Wang Mang biography carries the tale of poisoned wine. The two versions stand together, and history cannot decide. After his death Mang raised Xuan's great-great-grandson Ying as heir, styled "the Infant," took the regency, and two years later took the dynasty itself. Ping's annals survive, but no matter of state passed through his hands: he was the most complete puppet on the Western Han throne — brought in at nine, dead at fourteen, the five years between being a script written entirely by others.

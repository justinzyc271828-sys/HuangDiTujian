# Fu Jian (Jian), Founder of Former Qin · Principal Deeds

Fu Jian, styled Jianye, was a Di of Lüeyang, third son of Fu Hong. When Fu Hong was done to death by Later Zhao, Jian took command of his host. In the 6th year of Yonghe he led his men through the pass, broke Du Hong, and took Chang'an; in the 1st year of Huangshi he styled himself Heavenly King and Great Chanyu of the realm Great Qin with his capital at Chang'an; in the 2nd year he took the imperial throne. The foundations of Former Qin were laid by Jian.

In mid-Huangshi, Huan Wen of Eastern Jin campaigned north and entered the pass, reaching Bashan, and Guanzhong was shaken. Jian cleared the land of its grain and held his walls; Wen's army ran out of food and withdrew — "Jian, at ease, wore the invader out," and Guanzhong was saved. In five years he gathered the refugees, encouraged farming, and installed his officials, "the barbarian custom gradually taking on Chinese ways." He died in the 5th year of Huangshi, posthumously Emperor Jingming. His son Sheng succeeded, a cruel tyrant, and was soon slain by his nephew Fu Jian — the great enterprise of Former Qin passed to the nephew.

A Di who seized Guanzhong in the chaos and founded Former Qin, Jian showed real generalship in denying Huan Wen. He founded the state but did not live to perfect it; the tyrant Sheng and the rise of Fu Jian were the work of his successors. Former Qin became the strongest of the Sixteen States under Fu Jian — but its roots were planted in Jian's reign.

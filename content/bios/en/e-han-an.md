# Emperor An of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Hu was the son of the filial king Qing of Qinghe. In the first Yanping year, after the death of [[e-han-shang|Emperor Shang]], Empress Dowager Deng and her brother Deng Zhi decided behind closed doors to raise the thirteen-year-old Hu as Emperor An. His first fifteen years were the fifteen years of the Deng regency: the Yongchu edicts issued from the Deng house, and An himself was no more than a date in the annals.

His reign's gravest problem was the frontier. In the first Yongchu year the states of the Western Regions turned against Han and the protectorate was abolished; in the second Yongchu year the Xianling Qiang rose in force, Liangzhou was devastated, and year after year troops were raised until the treasury was empty — the "Yongchu Qiang wars" exhausted the accumulated strength of early Eastern Han. There was even a proposal to abandon Liangzhou, rejected only by the arguments of Yu Xu. The Deng regency could still hold the line; but in the first Jianguang year Empress Dowager Deng died, and An, at last ruling in person, at once destroyed Deng Zhi and the Deng house, turning instead to his wet nurse Wang Sheng and the eunuchs Jiang Jing and Fan Feng — the court grew more obstructed than ever.

In the third Yanguang year he deposed Crown Prince Bao as prince of Jiyin — a decision that planted the seed of the eunuch enthronement of [[e-han-shun|Emperor Shun]] a decade later. In the fourth Yanguang year An died at Ye during a southern tour, aged thirty-two. Empress Yan concealed the death, and with Jiang Jing and others raised the Marquis of Beixiang; when the marquis died the next year, the eunuch Sun Cheng and nineteen others enthroned the prince of Jiyin, and Eastern Han entered its middle-late phase of alternating eunuch and consort usurpation. The historians said of An that "though he sat on the throne and received sacrifice, his good name did not end well"; his years of personal rule mark the beginning of the dynasty's general decay.

# Emperor Yuan of Liang · Principal Deeds

Xiao Yi, styled Shicheng, was the seventh son of Emperor Wu, enfeoffed Prince of Xiangdong. Sharp and studious from youth, "learned in all the books," he wrote the *Jinlouzi* and gathered a library of 140,000 volumes — the greatest collection of the southern courts. In the Hou Jing storm, as Governor of Jingzhou, he joined Wang Sengbian against Jing; in the 1st year of Chengsheng Sengbian took Jiankang and destroyed Hou Jing, and Yi took the throne at Jiangling, changing the era to Chengsheng — the restoration of Liang, but a restoration seated at Jiangling instead of Jiankang, and that was the seed of disaster.

Three years he reigned: in the 2nd year of Chengsheng his brother the Prince of Wuling, Xiao Ji, took the throne in Shu, and Yuan destroyed him; but his jealousy of his kin was worse than Hou Jing's — he slew his brother the Prince of Shaoling, and the family slaughter outdid the rebellion. In the 3rd year of Chengsheng Western Wei sent Yujin and the others south on five roads and broke Jiangling; the emperor surrendered and was taken and slain, aged forty-seven. As the city fell, Yuan "burned all his books" — 140,000 volumes in one fire: the Jiangling book-burning, one of the great calamities of Chinese culture.

Sharp and studious but steeped in jealousy, the historians say "his error was not returning to Jiankang in time": with the rebellion ended, the Liang house should have stood on Jiankang, but Yuan hugged Jiangling and slew his kin until Western Wei struck through the gap. Liang fell not to Hou Jing but to Yuan's jealousy and his short sight; and the fire of Jiangling burned away the culture that a hundred years of the southern courts had built.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Liangshu*, ch. 5 ("Benji of Emperor Yuan"), and the *Zhoushu*, ch. 15 (biography of Yujin).

# Emperor Ming of Song · Principal Deeds

Liu Yu, styled Xiubing, was the eleventh son of Emperor Wen, enfeoffed Prince of Xiangdong. After the Former Deposed Emperor's slaying he took the throne and changed the era to Taishi. A reader and a master of clerical script, "a man of letters," he inherited the ruin: in the 2nd year of Taishi the Prince of Jin'an, Zixun, took the throne at Jiangzhou with half the realm answering, and the civil war ran over a year before the emperor, campaigning in person, put it down — the princes and ministers of Liu Song were nearly spent.

In the mid-Taishi years, with Wei in internal disorder, he campaigned north — but his generals quarreled, and Xu, Yan, Qing, and Ji fell to Wei: the Huai north was lost at a stroke, and the southern frontier has run along the Huai ever since — the hinge of the south's decline. In his last years he grew "full of taboos and jealous cruelty," slaying his brothers and his meritorious ministers against the perils to come; and, having no son, he secretly had his favorites "bear children" for the princes — the family slaughter of the age, carried further than ever.

He died in the 1st year of Taiyu at thirty-four; the Later Deposed Emperor Yu succeeded. The histories say: "jealous and cruel, worst of all in his last years." From Wu's founding to Ming, not fifty years, and kin slaying kin was the norm; with Ming's death Liu Song was spent — the Later Deposed Emperor was slain, Emperor Shun abdicated, and Xiao Daocheng took the realm.

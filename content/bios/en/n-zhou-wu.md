# Emperor Wu of Northern Zhou · Principal Deeds

Yuwen Yong, fourth son of Yuwen Tai, succeeded by Ming's will. At first he hid his purpose: Yuwen Hu had ruled twelve years, and Yong "kept himself obscure," serving Hu with care while his mind was set on the killing. In the 1st year of Jiande, plotting with his brother the Prince of Wei, he summoned Hu to the palace and struck him down before the empress dowager — "the emperor struck Hu from behind with the jade tablet, and Hu fell" — destroyed his party, and took up the government himself. The throne of Zhou was restored.

His rule ran on frugality and the Confucian way: in the 3rd year of Jiande he cut off Buddhism and Daoism, destroyed the scriptures and images, and sent monks and priests back to lay life — "the people's burdens were lightened," and the state's strength grew; he forbade luxury and trimmed the palaces, a frugality far from the southern courts. In the 6th year of Jiande he campaigned in person against Qi, took Ye, and captured the Last Sovereign Gao Wei — the north, split since the division of Wei, was one again, and Zhou stood at its greatest.

In the 1st year of Xuanzheng, on the campaign against the Turks, he fell ill on the road and died at Yunyang, aged thirty-six. "Heroic and frugal," he was the first sovereign of the later northern age; but his early death left the throne to the tyrant Xuan, and Yang Jian took the realm — the unity he won passed within years to Sui. The histories say: "of surpassing strategy, he united the north," but "his early death loosed the disaster" — Sui was Zhou's continuation.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Zhoushu*, ch. 5 and 6 ("Benji of Emperor Wu").

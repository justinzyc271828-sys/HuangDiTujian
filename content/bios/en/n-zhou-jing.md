# Emperor Jing of Northern Zhou · Principal Deeds

Yuwen Chan, eldest son of Xuan, first named Yan, took the throne in the 1st year of Daxiang at seven, with Yang Jian — father of the emperor's empress — as grand chancellor of the left, holding all the government. In the 2nd year of Daxiang Xuan died, and Jian, by forged edict, took the regency and cut down the strong princes of the Zhou house; that same year the governors Yuchi Jiong of Xiangzhou and Sima Xiaonan of Yunzhou rose against him, and Jian's generals, Wei Xiaokuan and the rest, destroyed them — the Zhou princes' resistance was spent in a day.

In the second month of the 1st year of Dading Jian received the abdication, styled the realm Sui, and Northern Zhou was no more. The former emperor, made Duke of Jie, was done to death in the fifth month, aged nine. From Xiaomin's abdication to Jing's, Zhou ran five sovereigns and twenty-five years; and Sui, taking the Zhou abdication, was its direct continuation — the institutions and the borders passed entire.

The histories say: "a child in the abdication, his life the foundation of Sui." A seven-year-old watching his grandfather's realm pass, not his own master. Zhou fell not to Jing but to Xuan's tyranny; Jing only inherited the disaster. And the Sui reunification, carrying Zhou's foundations into the age of Tang, shows how far the fallen Zhou still reached.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Zhoushu*, ch. 8 ("Benji of Emperor Jing"), and the *Suishu*, ch. 1 ("Benji of Emperor Wen").

# Emperor Wen of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Heng was a lesser son of Gaozu, enfeoffed as King of Dai with his seat at Zhongdu, where he lived seventeen years known for caution. In the eighth year of Empress Lü, with the Lü clan destroyed, the meritorious ministers summoned him: he hesitated at first, and only after persuasion from his attendants crossed the Wei Bridge, received the imperial seal, and entered the Weiyang Palace to take the throne. Entering the succession from an outer fief, he opened the twenty-three years known as the Wen-Jing recovery.

He governed by frugality, leniency, and benevolence: immediately on accession he repealed the law against slander and seditious speech, opening the channels of remonstrance; in the thirteenth year, moved by Tiying's petition, he abolished mutilation punishments and revised the law; that same year he remitted the land tax throughout the realm; once, planning a terrace, he abandoned it on learning it would cost a hundred jin of gold, saying, "A hundred jin is the estate of ten middle households." The historian's eulogy says that in twenty-three years he added nothing to his palaces, gardens, or parks, attending wholly to transforming the people by virtue. When the Xiongnu raided the frontier he wished to campaign in person, but the empress dowager stopped him; he relied on posted generals and the marriage alliance to hold the border — the scene of his visit to the camp at Xiliu leaving the famous exchange with the "true general" Zhou Yafu.

He died in the later seventh year; his will ordered brief mourning and a frugal burial for all the realm, and the Baling mound was hewn into the hillside — frugality maintained to the end. His heir [[han-jing-di|Emperor Jing of Han]] continued his course, hence the name "Wen-Jing recovery"; the foundations of Han's recuperation held until the reign of [[han-wu-di|Emperor Wu of Han]] turned to frontier expansion. Later historians ranked Wen with [[han-gao-zu|Emperor Gaozu]] and Emperor Jing, and after the Three Dynasties he became the standard of the good ruler.

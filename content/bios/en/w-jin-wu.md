# Emperor Wu of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Yan, styled Anshi, was the eldest son of Sima Zhao. In the twelfth month of the 2nd year of Xianxi he received the abdication of Wei and took the throne, changing the era to Taishi, styling the realm Jin, and making Luoyang his capital — the sixty-year division since the end of Han was on its way to an end. He began with purpose: he fixed the suburban and ancestral rites, issued the five articles to discipline local government, and in the 9th year of Taishi swept the daughters of the great houses into his harem — the beginnings of his luxury. In the 5th year of Xianning Ma Long struck down Tufa Shujineng, and Liangzhou was pacified.

In the 1st year of Taikang, with Jia Chong grand commander, Wang Jun, Wang Hun, and others came down on Wu from six roads; Wang Jun's fleet descended from Yizhou, Sun Hao surrendered bound and bare, and the Three Kingdoms were one under Jin. After the conquest he grew complacent: he disbanded the provincial armies, keeping a hundred armed clerks in the great commanderies; he enfeoffed the imperial princes on a grand scale to govern the four quarters, and the feudatories grew strong; his harem held nearly ten thousand ladies, and he rode a sheep-cart to let it choose where he slept — "the sheep-cart's favor." The nine-rank system continued, and the great clans waxed.

In the fourth month of the 1st year of Taixi the emperor died at fifty-five, and his dull-witted heir brought on the War of the Eight Princes. The historians credit him as "broad of bearing and shrewd in counsel," a sovereign worthy of the unification; but the three errors — disbanding the armies, enfeoffing the princes, favoring his wives' kin — sowed the ruin while the Taikang recovery flourished. Western Jin's fall, the historians agree, was prepared in his own reign.

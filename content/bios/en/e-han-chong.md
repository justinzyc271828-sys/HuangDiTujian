# Emperor Chong of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Bing was the son of [[e-han-shun|Emperor Shun]]. When Shun died in the first Jiankang year, the two-year-old Bing took the throne, with Empress Dowager Liang in the regency and Liang Ji in power — Eastern Han's child-emperor politics now reaching its extreme form, a sovereign too small even for the courtesy of dating.

He reigned about five months. In the first Yongjia year bandits rose in force at Jiujiang and elsewhere: the court faced an empire of spreading revolt, while the Liang house was busy consolidating its own position. In the first month of the year he died in the front hall of the Yutang Palace, having reigned under a year, and was buried at Huailing. Empress Dowager Liang and Liang Ji then chose Liu Zuan, son of the filial king of Bohai, as [[e-han-zhi|Emperor Zhi]].

No policy, no will, not even a settled era-name system of his own belongs to Chong's reign (Yongjia was the era adopted after Jiankang, its government all Liang's doing). His significance is that the Han throne had grown empty enough to be filled by an infant — and the next infant to be filled in, the eight-year-old [[e-han-zhi|Emperor Zhi]], would end himself with the phrase "arrogant general," opening the curtain on the Huan-Ling age.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Hou Hanshu*, ch. 6 (Benji of Emperor Chong) and the biography of Liang Ji; Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

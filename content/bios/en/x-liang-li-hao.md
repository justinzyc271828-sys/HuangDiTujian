# Li Gao, Founder of Western Liang · Principal Deeds

Li Gao, styled Xuansheng, was a man of Chengji in Longxi, claiming descent from the Han general Li Guang. "Studious from youth, learned in the classics and histories," he served as Duan Ye's governor of Dunhuang. In the 1st year of Gengzi he held Dunhuang and styled himself Duke of Liang, opening the era Gengzi — the beginning of Western Liang. In the 1st year of Jianchu he moved the capital to Jiuquan, "looking eastward," to contend for Hexi.

His rule honored letters: he founded an academy, enlarged the students to five hundred, encouraged farming and mulberries, and taught the people husbandry; "governing by culture," he made the letters of Hexi flourish. But Western Liang was cramped and weak, set between Northern Liang and the Rouran, its borders shrinking. He died in the 1st year of Jiaxing, posthumously King Wuzhao; his son Xin succeeded, fought Juqu Mengxun at Liaoquan, fell in battle, and Western Liang perished.

The historians say of him: "of outstanding bearing, with the air of a hero," yet "his land was not a hundred commanderies, his host not ten thousand men" — no match for Mengxun. Western Liang lasted twenty-one years; its government was admirable, but between strong neighbors culture could not hold against arms. His descendants served Northern Wei and passed into the Sui and Tang — the Tang founder Li Yuan claiming descent from King Wuzhao of Liang: the state died, but the Li line ran on to an empire.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 87 (biography of the Martial King of Liang), ch. 129 (records of Juqu Mengxun).

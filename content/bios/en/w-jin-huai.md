# Emperor Huai of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Chi, styled Fengdu, was the twenty-fifth son of Emperor Wu, a student of wide learning known for his quiet love of letters, enfeoffed Prince of Yuzhang. In the 1st year of Guangxi, on Emperor Hui's death, the Prince of Donghai brought him to the throne; the era was changed to Yongjia. He wished to act, but the War of the Eight Princes had barely ended and the Xiongnu storm was rising: the Prince of Donghai governed alone and the emperor folded his hands; Yue, marching out with the armies, died of worry at Xiang.

In the 4th year of Yongjia Liu Yuan died and Liu Cong succeeded, sending Liu Yao and Shi Le against Luoyang; the city starved until men ate men. In the sixth month of the 5th year, Liu Yao and Wang Mi broke in, burned and looted, and slew over thirty thousand officials and people; the emperor was carried off to Pingyang, where Liu Cong made him serve wine in servant's dress before putting him to death, aged thirty. The capital of Western Jin lay in ruins.

Six years on the throne, Chi was no man without spirit, but he inherited the wreck of the princes' war with the barbarian cavalry at the gate; there was no room for recovery. The Yongjia disaster and the "crossing of the caps and gowns south" mark the fall of Western Jin: with Luoyang in ashes and the Central Plains lost, the Jin house fled south, and the age of the Eastern Jin and the Five Barbarian Sixteen States began.

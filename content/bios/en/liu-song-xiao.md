# Emperor Xiaowu of Song · Principal Deeds

Liu Jun, styled Xiulong, was the third son of Emperor Wen. In the 30th year of Yuanjia the heir Liu Shao slew Emperor Wen; Jun, the Prince of Wuling holding Jiangzhou, rose against him, destroyed Shao and his party, and took the throne, changing the era to Xiaojian — the "parricide" destroyed, and the age applauded. But his rule turned harsh: in the 1st year of Xiaojian he put down the Prince of Nanjun's revolt; in the 3rd year of Daming the Prince of Jingling rose at Guangling, and Jun, having destroyed him, massacred the city — "all the male inhabitants were slain" — the bitterest massacre of the southern dynasties.

Eleven years on the throne, he raised buildings endlessly, changing his palaces and dress, "keeping his own store," and the levies crushed the people; he distrusted the princes and many were done to death. Yet there was method: he clipped the princes' power, gathered the governors' authority to the center, and set the "secretaries of inspection" over the princely courts — a new institution of southern dynastic politics.

He died in the 8th year of Daming at thirty-five; the Former Deposed Emperor Ziye succeeded. The histories say he was "sharp from youth, of brilliant bearing," yet "trusting his own ability, he slew without measure." The slaughters of the southern palace grew worse from his reign on: he won the throne by avenging a parricide, and his son died a tyrant's death — the family wars of Liu Song could not be stopped.

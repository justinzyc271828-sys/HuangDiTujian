# Emperor Xuanzong of Tang (the Second) · Principal Deeds

Li Chen, first named Yi, was the thirteenth son of Xianzong. On Wuzong's death in the 6th year of Huichang the eunuchs set up Chen, changing the era to Dazhong. "Outwardly weak, inwardly sharp of scrutiny," on accession he exiled Li Deyu to Yazhou and the Niu–Li factional balance shifted; he "judged sharply," disciplined the officials, and checked the eunuchs — the government of Tang was steadied for a moment, and the histories call his reign the Dazhong recovery, honoring him as the "Little Taizong."

In the 3rd year of Dazhong, Zhang Yichao of Shazhou rose and submitted to Tang, recovering eleven prefectures of the Hehuang — part of the Longyou lands lost to Tibet since the An–Shi rebellion returned; the Guiyi Army was set to hold it. But Xuanzong "saw clearly into everything": he distrusted his ministers — "the courtiers dared not look up when they spoke" — deposed his heir, and in his last years took the elixirs.

He died in the 13th year of Dazhong, aged fifty. The histories say: "he judged sharply, saw clearly, and the Tang house enjoyed a small peace." The Dazhong recovery was the last modest ease of the Tang's last years; but the "Little Taizong" only kept the realm, and could not root out the feudatories and the eunuchs. After his death came Yizong's extravagance and Xizong's flight — the Dazhong recovery was the Tang's last glow.

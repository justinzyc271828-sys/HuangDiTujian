# Emperor An of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Dezong, eldest son of Emperor Xiaowu by Lady Chen, was "unfortunate — from childhood to manhood he could not speak, and could not tell heat from cold or hunger from fullness," the dullest of the Eastern Jin sovereigns. In the 21st year of Taiyuan, Emperor Xiaowu slain, Dezong took the throne at fifteen, with Sima Daozi and his son Yuanxian governing.

Twenty-two years he sat while the dynasty was turned upside down: in the 3rd year of Long'an Sun En rose against Kuajji and the south was in turmoil; in the 1st year of Yuanxing Huan Xuan entered the capital and slew Yuanxian; in the 2nd year of Yuanxing Xuan degraded the emperor to Prince of Pinggu and founded his realm of Chu — the first fall of Eastern Jin to a usurper. In the 3rd year of Yuanxing Liu Yu rose at Jingkou, destroyed Xuan, and restored the emperor; the era was changed to Yixi. In the 5th year of Yixi Yu destroyed Southern Yan, took Guanggu, and beheaded Murong Chao; in the 6th he broke Lu Xun; from the 12th to the 13th he campaigned against Later Qin, entering Luoyang and taking Chang'an — the greatest military glory of Eastern Jin, and all of it Liu Yu's, none of it the emperor's.

In the 14th year of Yixi, in haste for the abdication, Liu Yu had the emperor strangled in the eastern hall and set up his brother Dewen as Emperor Gong. A mindless man on the throne for twenty-two years, Dezong watched Huan Xuan's usurpation and Liu Yu's rise without ever knowing them — Eastern Jin's rise and fall passed him by. The historians say: "Unfortunate, he left all power to the ministers." That such a sovereign prolonged the Jin line twenty years was the accident of the strong ministers' rivalry; the moment Liu Yu made up his mind, the mandate passed.

# Emperor Ruizong of Tang (First Reign) · Principal Deeds

Li Dan, first named Xulun, was the eighth son of Gaozong by Empress Wu. After Zhongzong's deposition in the 1st year of Sisheng, Wu set up the Prince of Yu, Dan, changing the era to Wenming — Ruizong's first reign. Dan was "frugal and yielding": on the throne "every matter was decided by Empress Wu," and the emperor folded his hands; in the 1st year of Guangzhai, Xu Jingye rose at Yangzhou against Wu and was soon destroyed — Wu's authority was fixed.

Through the Chuigong, Yongchang, and Zaichu years Wu moved toward the reality of abdication: in the 1st year of Zaichu she took the throne, renamed the realm Zhou, and Dan was reduced to heir of the line, given the surname Wu — "reduced to heir," the Tang imperial line interrupted and the Zhou established. Six years of his first reign, in truth the transition of Wu's regency: in name he held the Tang line, but "a puppet emperor on the eve of the Wu Zhou."

His enthronement and his reduction were none of his choosing; his quiet yielding kept him safe. His restoration after the Tanglong coup is recorded under "Emperor Ruizong of Tang (Restored)."

# Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei · Key Events

Yuan Hong (born into the Tuoba clan) is a pivot of institutional reform that no reader of the Northern and Southern Dynasties can bypass. He ascended the throne as a child at Pingcheng in Yanxing 1 (471), with Empress Dowager Wenming (Feng) ruling as regent in his early years. This page takes 《魏书·高祖纪》（卷007上下） as the backbone of its chronology, cross-checked against the empresses' biographies (皇后传) and the Treatise on Food and Money (食货志) in the Book of Wei, and against the corresponding annals of 《资治通鉴》.

During the Taihe era, the salary system (Taihe 8) paid officials to reward integrity and discipline the bureaucracy; the equal-field system (Taihe 9) was promulgated and would deeply shape Sui–Tang debates on land tenure; and the three-elders system (Taihe 10) rebuilt grassroots administration. Together these Taihe New Policies formed the institutional skeleton of the reform.

In Taihe 17 (493), under the pretext of a southern campaign, he moved the capital to Luoyang, shifting Northern Wei's political center south. There followed the ban on Hu dress and speech, the fixing of aristocratic clan rankings, and the change of the Tuoba surname to Yuan—sinicization rolled out in full. The costs were equally clear: in Taihe 20 (496), Crown Prince Yuan Xun, who had resisted sinicization, was deposed, shaking the palace.

In his final years he campaigned persistently on the Xiangyang front, draining the state's strength; in Taihe 23 (499) he died in the army during a southern campaign and was buried back at Luoyang. The sinicization line was carried on by Xuanwu, while the controversies of radical reform and the tensions with the Six Garrisons were sown here as well. Read alongside [[n-wei-taiwu|Emperor Taiwu of Northern Wei]]: one first completed the unification of the north by arms, the other remade the dynasty's face through institutional reform—the two faces of Northern Wei meet and part in these two men.

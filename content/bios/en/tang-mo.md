# The Last Sovereign of Later Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Congke, originally of the Wang surname, was the adopted son of Mingzong. In the 1st year of Qingtai (934) he rose at Fengxiang, deposed the Min emperor, and took the throne, changing the era to Qingtai. His throne was won by mutiny and ill-gotten; but he was "strong and a fine rider and archer," and his early government could still employ the worthy. In the 2nd year of Qingtai, however, he came to distrust the Hedong governor Shi Jingtang — the transfers and stripping of garrisons that drove Jingtang to revolt.

In the 3rd year of Qingtai (936) Jingtang took the throne at Taiyuan, called on the Khitan, acknowledged the Khitan ruler Yelü Deguang as father, and ceded the Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun; the Khitan host came south and the Tang armies were beaten again and again. That eleventh month the Khitan and Jin armies entered Luoyang; the Last Sovereign climbed the Xuanwu Tower and burned himself — Later Tang was no more, aged fifty-two. From Zhuangzong's destruction of Liang to the Last Sovereign's fire, four emperors and fourteen years.

The histories say: "his throne was ill-gotten, and he fell to the Khitan at last." Later Tang fell to Jingtang's revolt and the Khitan's strength; and the cession of the Sixteen Prefectures was a disaster for China for four hundred years — from Liao and Jin to the Mongols, the northern menace ran from this. The Last Sovereign, dying in his own fire, kept at least the fire of a fallen king.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 46–48 ("Annals of the Tang Last Sovereign").

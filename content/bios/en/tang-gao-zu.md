# Emperor Gaozu of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Yuan, styled Shude, was a man of Chengji in Longxi, son of the Duke of Tang, Li Bing. Of the Guanzhong aristocracy of Northern Zhou, he "succeeded to the dukedom at seven," and in the last years of Sui held Taiyuan. In the 13th year of Daye, with the realm in chaos, Yuan rose at Taiyuan — urged on by his second son Shimin and the others — and turned west toward Guanzhong; taking Chang'an, he set up the Prince of Dai, Yang You, as emperor, and made himself grand chancellor and King of Tang — "holding the emperor to command the lords," the power of Sui passing to the Li house.

In the 1st year of Wude, Emperor Gong abdicated; Yuan took the throne, styled the realm Tang, changed the era, and fixed his capital at Chang'an. The Wude years pacified the heroes: in the 4th year Shimin broke Wang Shichong and Dou Jiande at Luoyang, and Henan and Hebei were roughly settled; Liu Heita, Xiao Xian, Fu Gongshi, and the rest fell in turn, and the realm drew toward unity. The institutions of the Tang — the equal-field and tax systems, the garrison system — were roughed out in the Wude years.

In the 9th year of Wude came the Xuanwu Gate: Shimin slew the heir Jiancheng and the Prince of Qi, Yuanji, and Yuan, compelled, yielded the throne to Shimin, styling himself Grand Emperor. He died in the Tai'an Palace in the 9th year of Zhenguan, aged seventy. The histories say he "founded the Tang house with its capital in Guanzhong," yet "irresolute, his yielding of the throne bound up with the Xuanwu Gate." The three hundred years of Tang were laid open by Gaozu's Wude; and his yielding made Taizong's reign possible.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 1 ("Benji of Emperor Gaozu"), and the *Xin Tangshu*, ch. 1.

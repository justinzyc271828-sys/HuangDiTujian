# The Emperor Chu of Later Jin · Principal Deeds

Shi Chonggui, son of Jingtang's elder brother, was made heir. On Gaozu's death in the 7th year of Tianfu he took the throne, changing the era to Kaiyun. The Emperor Chu was "proud from youth," and on accession Jing Yangguang and the others urged war: envoys told the Khitan, "grandson, but not vassal" — refusing the rites of submission. The Liao ruler Yelü Deguang, in fury at the "ingratitude of Jin," marched south in force.

In the Kaiyun years Jin and Liao fought their great wars: the Khitan came south three times, and the Jin armies won at first (at Majiakou, at Baituanwei), but the generals quarreled and the supplies failed; in the 4th year of Kaiyun (947) Deguang led the Liao host into Kaifeng, and the Emperor Chu, unable to fight or hold, surrendered bare-armed and was taken north — Later Jin was no more, two emperors and eleven years. He died in exile at Jianzhou in Khitan lands.

The histories say: "he could neither serve the Khitan humbly nor stand on his own." A son-emperor's realm seeking to defy the Khitan with insufficient strength — "to fight, strength wanting; to make peace, the name already broken" — he ended in national ruin and personal captivity. The fall of Jin was the bitterest of the Five Dynasties: the Central Plains stood masterless, the Khitan entered Kaifeng for a time, and Liu Zhiyuan seized the chance to found Han.

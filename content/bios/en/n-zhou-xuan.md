# Emperor Xuan of Northern Zhou · Principal Deeds

Yuwen Yun, eldest son of Emperor Wu, took the throne on his father's death in the 1st year of Xuanzheng. On the throne he "gave himself to debauchery and arrogance," reversing Wu's course: he styled himself the "Heavenly Emperor Tianyuan," comparing himself to Heaven, dwelling in the "Terrace of Heaven" where all petitions had to speak of Heaven; he set up four empresses at once, the "Heavenly Grand Empress" and the rest standing together — the rites of Zhou fell apart.

He punished and slew without measure: on accession he slew his uncle the Prince of Qi, Yuwen Xian, then the meritorious Wang Gui and the others; "he beat men to a hundred and twenty strokes," the tortures cruel. In the 1st year of Daxiang he abdicated to his heir Yuwen Chan but, styling himself the Tianyuan Emperor, "still took part in the government," more extravagant than ever; he died in the 2nd year of Daxiang at twenty-two — two years of tyranny had spent the realm.

After his death the child Jing took the throne and Yang Jian, by forged edict, the regency — and soon the abdication: Northern Zhou fell to Xuan's tyranny. The histories say: "the Tianyuan's excess destroyed his own realm." The unity won by Emperor Wu was thrown away within ten years; Zhou rose swiftly and fell swiftly, and Xuan's darkness wrote its ending.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Zhoushu*, ch. 7 ("Benji of Emperor Xuan").

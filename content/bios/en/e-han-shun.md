# Emperor Shun of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Bao was the son of [[e-han-an|Emperor An]]. Made heir apparent in the first Yongning year, he was deposed by slander as prince of Jiyin in the third Yanguang year. In the fourth Yanguang year the Marquis of Beixiang died, and the eunuch Sun Cheng with eighteen others slew Jiang Jing and enthroned the eleven-year-old prince of Jiyin as Emperor Shun — the first succession in Chinese imperial history accomplished entirely by eunuchs.

The structure of his reign was set from that moment: in the first year the nineteen eunuchs were enfeoffed on the same day, and the eunuchs became an independent political force. The consorts did not leave the stage: in the first Yangjia year Liang Na was made empress, and Liang Shang and then Liang Ji held the office of commander-in-chief; in the sixth Yonghe year Liang Shang died and Liang Ji succeeded — the most overbearing consort of Eastern Han now formally in power.

On the frontier, in the second Yongjian year Ban Yong struck the Xiongnu's Huyan king and the states of the Western Regions were reopened — one of the few deeds of Shun's reign worth the name; in the first Han'an year he sent Du Qiao, Zhou Ju, and six others to tour the provinces and inspect the officials, showing a will to reform that came to nothing against the larger drift. In the first Jiankang year Emperor Shun died at thirty. He died at the summit of Liang power, leaving a two-year-old [[e-han-chong|Emperor Chong]] and the legacy summed up by the historians: "in the reign of Shun, power returned to the consort clan."

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Hou Hanshu*, ch. 6 (Benji of Emperor Shun), and the biographies of the Eunuchs, Ban Yong, and Liang Ji; Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

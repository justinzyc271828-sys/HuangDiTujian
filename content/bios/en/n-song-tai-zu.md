# Emperor Taizu of Song · Major Events

Zhao Kuangyin was originally a general of the Later Zhou palace armies and an old subordinate of [[zhou-shi|Emperor Shizong of Later Zhou]]. In the first year of Jianlong (960), the mutiny at Chenqiao Station draped him in the yellow robe; he replaced the Zhou and founded the Song, fixing the capital at Kaifeng — a new dynasty standing atop the accumulated habits of the Five Dynasties had made its entrance. Whether the mutiny was premeditated is disputed in the sources and is left open here.

The first hard problem after the founding was military command. In the second year of Jianlong (961), he relieved the veteran generals of the palace armies of their commands over a wine banquet — the so-called "release of military power over a cup of wine." Its details have been much embellished by later generations, but the direction of concentrating elite troops and dividing authority was set from then on. Together with arrangements that elevated civil officials and split up military power, "strengthening the trunk and weakening the branches" and "exalting the civil and restraining the military" became the Song dynasty's long-term path of state power, with an influence reaching far beyond Taizu's own reign.

The wars of unification followed a "south first, north later" strategy: in the fourth year of Jianlong (963), Murong Yanzhao and others pacified Jinghu; in the third year of Qiande (965), Wang Quanbin and others destroyed Later Shu; in the fourth year of Kaibao (971), Pan Mei and others destroyed Southern Han; and in the eighth year of Kaibao (975), Cao Bin pacified Jiangnan, where [[n-tang-houzhu|Li Yu, Last Ruler of Southern Tang]] surrendered. The southern states entered the Song one after another, and the fragmentation left over from the Five Dynasties was largely brought to a close.

In the ninth year of Kaibao (976), Taizu died at Kaifeng, and his younger brother, Emperor Taizong of Song, took the throne. "The shadow of a candle and the sound of an axe" and the "Golden Coffer Pact" have long been the disputed layers of this succession; this page points to them without settling them as fact.

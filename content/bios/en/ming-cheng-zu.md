# Zhu Di, Emperor Chengzu of Ming · Major Deeds

Zhu Di (1360-1424), fourth son of [[ming-tai-zu|Emperor Taizu]], Prince of Yan, guarded Beiping. In 1399 he rose in the "Jingnan" war, commanding his armies in person before taking the throne, and entered Nanjing in 1402 as emperor, era Yongle.

His reign was one of arms and splendor: five personal campaigns into the steppe against the remnants of the Yuan; Zheng He's seven voyages reaching East Africa; the capital moved to Beijing, "the Son of Heaven guarding the gate." In government he founded the Grand Secretariat and compiled the Yongle Encyclopedia. Yet he won by usurpation, executed Fang Xiaoru and the Jianwen loyalists, and punished their kin to the fourth degree - a stain never washed away.

He died at Yumuchuan in 1424, returning from the north. Rarely has an emperor combined such genius with such a tainted claim.

# Tuoba Shiyijian, King of Dai · Principal Deeds

Tuoba Shiyijian was a chief of the Tuoba Xianbei, son of Tuoba Yulü. In his youth he was a hostage in Later Zhao and saw the institutions of the Central Plains. In the 1st year of Jianguo he took the throne as King of Dai, opened the era Jianguo, and made Shengle in Yunzhong his capital — the Dai realm now had its own era and capital, and began to leave the tribal way behind.

His reign marks the passage of the Tuoba from tribal league to state: he first fixed the laws, "clear and strict"; he allotted the lands for settlement and "built walled towns"; he installed his officials on the Jin model. In arms he campaigned north again and again against the Gaoche and west against Liu Weichen, and the north grew strong under him. In the 39th year of Jianguo, Fu Jian of Former Qin came against him in force; the tribes scattered, and Shiyijian was slain by his own son Shijun (or, some say, by Fu Jian's men) — the Dai realm was no more.

But the Tuoba root was not cut: his grandson Tuoba Gui, after the Fei River defeat, restored the state under the name Wei — Emperor Daowu of Northern Wei. Northern Wei honored Shiyijian as Emperor Zhaocheng. The historians see in him the maker of the laws and statehood of the Tuoba, the forerunner of Northern Wei: of all the barbarian states, only the Tuoba fell and rose again to unite the north — and the foundation was laid by Shiyijian.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 1 ("Prefatory Annals"), ch. 113 (treatise on official clans).

# The Former Young Emperor · Principal Deeds

Liu Gong, the Former Young Emperor, was the child ruler raised by Empress Lü under the fiction of being a son of the harem of [[han-hui-di|Emperor Hui of Han]]. After Hui died, Lü took the regency and enthroned one of his harem sons to continue the Han line, while all real power rested with the Lü house. The legitimacy of the arrangement was doubtful from the start — the court knew the boy was probably not of Hui's blood, and the histories record his reign not in his own annals but in the "Basic Annals of Empress Lü."

His four years were the apex of Lü power: in the first year of the regency the Lü were enfeoffed as kings, breaking the White Horse Covenant of [[han-gao-zu|Emperor Gaozu]], and the laws were adjusted to the regency's taste. The young emperor was no more than a date in the annals.

Growing up, he learned he was not the empress's son and spoke resentfully; in the fourth year of the regency he was imprisoned and slain, and Liu Hong, king of Hengshan, was enthroned in his place — the Later Young Emperor. Within four years the Han throne changed hands twice, both times at Lü's pleasure, until the accession of [[han-wen-di|Emperor Wen]] returned real sovereignty to the Liu house. The histories leave the Former Young Emperor's legitimacy an open question; this gazetteer therefore lists him as a quasi-emperor.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Shiji*, "Basic Annals of Empress Lü," and the *Hanshu*, ch. 3 (Benji of Empress Lü); Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

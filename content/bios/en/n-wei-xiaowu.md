# Emperor Xiaowu of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Xiu, third son of the Prince of Guangping, Yuan Huai, was enfeoffed Prince of Pingyang. In the 2nd year of Putai, Gao Huan deposed Emperor Jiemin and set up Xiu, changing the era to Taichang. Xiaowu "was grave and of real measure," but would not be Gao Huan's puppet: Huan sat at Jinyang ruling from afar, and the emperor, plotting with his intimates to destroy Huan, was discovered; in the 3rd year of Yongxi he rode out of Luoyang with a light escort, fled west to Chang'an, and leaned on the great governor of Guanzhong, Yuwen Tai — Northern Wei split into east and west.

In the pass he changed the era to Yongxi; but Yuwen Tai's grip was no lighter than Gao Huan's: as the emperor tried to stand alone, the breach with Tai widened. That same year, in the twelfth month, Tai had him poisoned at Chang'an, aged twenty-five. Gao Huan, on word of the poisoning, set up the heir of the Prince of Qinghe, Yuan Shanjian, and moved the capital to Ye — Eastern Wei began; Yuwen Tai set up Yuan Baoju at Chang'an — Western Wei began. Northern Wei, a hundred and forty-nine years from Daowu's founding, was formally split.

The histories say: "he sat at the split of Wei, fled west, and came to no good end." His resolve not to be a puppet drove him west to Tai, hoping to stand by the power of Guanzhong; but "he entered the tiger's den to fall to the wolf," and the Yuwen house destroyed him. His death ended the unity of Wei: East and West Wei, then Northern Qi and Northern Zhou, carried the realm into a new age of division.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 11 ("Benji of the Emperor Who Departed"), and the *Beishi*, ch. 5 (Wei basic annals).

# The Duke of Gaogui District · Principal Deeds

Cao Mao, styled Yanshi, was a grandson of Emperor Wen, son of Cao Lin, Prince Ding of Donghai, enfeoffed Duke of Gaogui District. In the 6th year of Jiaping, after Cao Fang's deposition, Sima Shi installed Mao at thirteen, changing the era to Zhengyuan. He was a student with an adult's gravity, debating the classics with the scholars at court, earning the name of a philosopher-sovereign; but the government lay with the Simas. In the 2nd year of Zhengyuan Guanqiu Jian and Wen Qin rose against Shi; in the 2nd year of Ganlu Zhuge Dan rose from Shouchun against Sima Zhao — both rose and both fell without result.

In the fifth month of the 5th year of Ganlu, with Sima Zhao's ambition plain, the emperor summoned Wang Shen, Wang Jing, and the others and said: "Sima Zhao's heart is known to every man on the road. I cannot sit and await disgrace — today I shall go out against him myself." He sallied forth with his palace guards, servants, and lads against Sima Zhao. Jia Chong of Zhao's party ordered Cheng Ji to strike; the emperor was slain beneath the southern gate, aged twenty. That an emperor should charge the spears in person was unheard of — the deed is recorded in the *Han–Jin Annals*, preserved in Pei Songzhi's commentary.

After the slaying, Sima Zhao blamed the emperor by the empress dowager's edict, degraded him to commoner, and installed Cao Huan, marquis of Changdao District. Posterity judges Cao Mao: of sharp spirit in a doomed position, he acted knowing he could not win — his constancy is pitiable, his resolve admirable. Of the Three Kingdoms' emperors, from Emperor Xian on, all clung to life; only the Duke of Gaogui District died for his altars. "Sima Zhao's heart" has passed into the language as the verdict of the people.

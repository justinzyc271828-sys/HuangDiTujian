# Emperor Ming of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Shao, styled Daoji, was the eldest son of Emperor Yuan by Lady Xun, and sharp from childhood. Emperor Yuan once set him on his knee and asked whether the sun or Chang'an was farther; "the sun," he answered. Asked again the next day he reversed himself: "the sun is farther — I can lift my head and see the sun, but not Chang'an." In the 1st year of Yongchang Yuan died and Shao took the throne at twenty-four, changing the era to Taining.

His reign opened with Wang Dun holding Wuchang, overbearing to the last degree. Ming had strategy: in the 1st year of Taining he made Wang Dao regent to reassure the Wangs; he himself, in disguise, rode out and viewed Dun's camps. In the 2nd year of Taining, with Dun dying of illness, the emperor campaigned in person, and Wen Qiao, Yu Liang, and the others broke Dun's party; Dun died as his army collapsed, and Jiangdong was at peace — the great villain cut down and the court's authority restored.

In the intercalary month of the 3rd year of Taining he died at twenty-seven. The histories call him "devoted to his parents, gifted in arms and letters," the strongest of the Eastern Jin sovereigns, a young emperor who put down a rebel minister in person. His early death left a five-year-old heir, Empress Yu's regency, and Yu Liang's government — and the Su Jun revolt. His brilliance and his brevity together shaped the pattern of Eastern Jin: a child on the throne, strong ministers rising.

# The Deposed Emperor of Northern Qi · Principal Deeds

Gao Yin, eldest son of Emperor Wenxuan, took the throne on his father's death in the 10th year of Tianbao at fifteen, changing the era to Qianming. The Deposed Emperor was "studious from youth and handsome," and his mother, Empress Li, was a Han — his accession was backed by the Han gentry, Yang Yin and the rest, who meant to check the Xianbei nobles.

But the nobles' roots ran deep: his uncles the Princes of Changshan and Zhangguang, Gao Yan and Gao Zhan, were Xianbei in spirit and held the Han in contempt. In the 1st year of Qianming they staged their coup, slew Yang Yin, Yan Zixian, and the others, and deposed Yin as Prince of Jinan; in the 1st year of Huangjian Yan took the throne himself; in the 2nd year Yan had Yin done to death at Jinyang, aged seventeen.

His deposition began the "uncle taking the nephew's throne" of Northern Qi: Xiaozhao and Wucheng in turn usurped as princes, the throne fell into the Xianbei nobles' hands, and the Han gentry's power was broken at a stroke. The histories say: "studious from youth, unable to hold his own." A fifteen-year-old before strong uncles, his deposition and his death were not his choosing — and the decline of Qi quickened from his fall.

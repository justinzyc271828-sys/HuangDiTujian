# Meng Zhixiang, Emperor Gaozu of Later Shu · Principal Deeds

Meng Zhixiang, a man of Xingzhou, was military governor of Xichuan under Later Tang. When Tang destroyed Former Shu in the 3rd year of Tongguang (925), Zhixiang became governor of Chengdu and Xichuan — the lands of Former Shu passed to Later Tang. In mid-Changxing, at odds with the court, he held Shu for himself; he destroyed Dong Zhang of the East Chuan and held both Chuan.

In the 1st year of Mingde (934) he took the imperial throne as the realm Shu (Later Shu to history), with its capital at Chengdu. But he reigned only months, dying at sixty-one; his son Meng Chang succeeded.

The histories say: "a rough founding; his reign was the briefest." Later Shu was a child of the Later Tang disorders: a governor seizing Shu from the chaos of the Central Plains, on the same road as Wang Jian of Former Shu. Brief as his reign was, his ordering and his choice of men laid the base of Meng Chang's age of culture and peace.

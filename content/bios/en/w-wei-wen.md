# Emperor Wen of Western Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Baoju, son of the Prince of Jingzhao, Yuan Yu, was a grandson of Emperor Xiaowen. In the 3rd year of Yongxi, after Xiaowu was poisoned at Chang'an, Yuwen Tai set up Baoju, with his capital at Chang'an, changing the era to Datong — the beginning of Western Wei. Seventeen years he reigned as Tai's puppet: in the 3rd year of Datong Tai broke Gao Huan at Shayuan and Western Wei stood; in the 9th year the Mangshan battle left fortunes divided — the standoff of East and West was fixed.

All the government of Western Wei came from Tai: he set up the fubing system, raising the six and twenty-four armies, "recruiting widely the strong houses of Guanzhong and Longxi to swell the ranks," and governed by the "six articles" — the seed of the institutions of Northern Zhou, Sui, and Tang. Wen "reigned with folded hands," every matter decided by Tai. He died in the 17th year of Datong at forty-five; his son Qin succeeded.

The histories say of him: "frugal, benevolent, a sovereign of preservation." Cramped in Guanzhong, Western Wei held Eastern Wei at bay through Tai's management; and Wen, though powerless, gave the emerging Guanzhong bloc a stable shell of imperial legitimacy — the foundations of Northern Zhou, Sui, and Tang were laid in Western Wei.

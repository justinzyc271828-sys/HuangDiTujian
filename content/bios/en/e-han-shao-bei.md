# The Marquis of Beixiang · Principal Deeds

Liu Yi, Marquis of Beixiang, was a son of the king of Jibei. In the third month of the fourth Yanguang year, [[e-han-an|Emperor An]] died at Ye on his southern tour; Empress Yan, her brother Yan Xian, and the eunuch Jiang Jing settled the succession in secret, concealed the death, and raised Liu Yi to the throne upon their return to the palace — yet another enthronement engineered by the harem and the consorts.

He reigned about two hundred days before dying, and the histories did not even give him a proper benji: his existence is recorded mainly in the gap between the end of An's annals and the beginning of Shun's. All government issued from the Yan house; Yan Xian and his brothers with Jiang Jing dominated the court, while the emperor himself has no deeds whatever to his name.

In the tenth month the Marquis of Beixiang died. The Yans concealed the death, intending to raise another king; but the palace eunuch Sun Cheng and eighteen others struck first, slew Jiang Jing, and enthroned the deposed heir, the prince of Jiyin [[e-han-shun|Liu Bao]], as Emperor Shun; Empress Yan was moved out of the palace and the Yan clan fell. The marquis's two hundred days of "reign" were the stage scenery for the showdown between the Yan faction and the eunuchs — and the last quasi-emperor enthronement of doubtful standing in Eastern Han.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Hou Hanshu*, ch. 6 (Benji of Emperor Shun), ch. 5 (Emperor An), the Annals of the Empresses, and the biography of the Eunuchs; Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

# Zhao Gou, Emperor Gaozong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Gou, ninth son of [[n-song-hui|Emperor Huizong]], held the title Prince Kang. After the two emperors were taken north in 1127 he was enthroned at Yingtian (modern Shangqiu), era Jianyan - the founding of Southern Song.

The Jianyan years were a desperate flight. The Miao-Liu mutiny forced his temporary abdication; when Jin troops advanced he fled to sea, drifting along the Zhejiang coast. In 1138 he fixed the temporary capital at Lin'an (modern Hangzhou), settling the southern state. In 1140 [[yue-fei|Yue Fei]] and others won victories at Yingchang and Yancheng - until Gaozong recalled the armies with "twelve gold edicts." In 1141 the Shaoxing Treaty made Song a vassal along the Huai River with annual tribute; Yue Fei was imprisoned and executed. The treaty brought twenty years of peace, but the "trumped-up charge" remains an eternal grievance.

In 1162 Gaozong abdicated to his adopted son Zhao Shen, [[s-song-xiao|Emperor Xiaozong]], and reigned on as retired emperor for twenty-five years, dying in 1187.

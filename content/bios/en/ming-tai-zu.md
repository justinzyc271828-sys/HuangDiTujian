# Zhu Yuanzhang, Emperor Taizu of Ming · Major Deeds

Zhu Yuanzhang (1328-1398), a poor peasant of Haozhou, lost his parents and brother to famine and plague, took refuge in a monastery, and wandered as a mendicant. In 1352 he joined Guo Zixing's Red Turban army, rose by valor and cunning, took Jiqing (Nanjing), destroyed Chen Youliang, crushed Zhang Shicheng, and received Fang Guozhen's surrender - and in 1368 took the imperial title at Yingtian as founder of the Ming, era Hongwu.

He ruled with harsh law: the Secretariat and chancellorship were abolished, all power gathering in the throne; the Embroidered Uniform Guard was founded; the Hu Weiyong and Lanyu cases swept away tens of thousands, nearly all the meritorious generals. Yet he also disciplined officials, surveyed land, opened farmland, and lightened taxes - the Hongwu years revived a war-torn realm.

He died at Nanjing in 1398, posthumously Taizu. From the meanest birth to the empire, diligent and cruel, his legacy defines the Ming.

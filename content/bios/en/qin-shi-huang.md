# Qin Shi Huang · Principal Deeds

As king of Qin, Ying Zheng built on the military-agricultural strength and reforms accumulated over several reigns to annex, one after another, the states east of the mountains, and in 221 BC he took the title "First Emperor" (Shi Huangdi). His significance lies not merely in "winning the wars," but in pushing institutions such as "emperor + commanderies and counties + a unified script and uniform axle widths" to an unprecedented scale.

After unification he toured incessantly (see the timeline and the sidebar map): east to the sea, south to the Xiang River, north to the frontier walls, proclaiming by his own presence that there was "one man over All-under-Heaven." The Feng and Shan sacrifices at Mount Tai and the stone inscriptions extolling his merit were the ritual language of power; at the same time, severe laws, massive construction projects, and thought control made "Qin governance" an enduring object of debate for later ages.

After the coup at Shaqiu, the throne fell to [[qin-er-shi|Qin Er Shi, the Second Emperor of Qin]], and the empire quickly lost stability. A few years later, the new political force represented by Liu Bang, [[han-gao-zu|Emperor Gaozu of Han]], took control of Guanzhong, yet Qin's institutional legacy entered the Han order in a more flexible form. The Feng and Shan rites and frontier expansion of later rulers such as [[han-wu-di|Emperor Wu of Han]] may also be read against this page over the long term.

# Emperor Wen of Sui · Major Deeds

Yang Jian replaced Northern Zhou and founded the Sui through an abdication, changing the era name to Kaihuang. With the new dynasty just established, he used political means to remove the lingering threat of the Northern Zhou royal house and steady the situation, while building and moving to Daxing and redesigning the capital's layout — the founding's first steps were taken with caution and thoroughness.

In Kaihuang 7, the Later Liang at Jiangling fell and the gate to the southern advance swung open; in Kaihuang 9 Chen was pacified, ending the nearly three-hundred-year division of north and south since the Eastern Jin — known in history as "the unification of the realm". The following year the anti-Sui forces in Jiangnan were suppressed, and the unified order was truly consolidated. Though the destruction of Chen was executed by his generals, every strategic decision came from the court.

Beyond unification, the Kaihuang era saw the Three Departments and Six Ministries take shape, currency and weights and measures unified, and households registered and checked — laying the institutional foundation of government for centuries to come, with exceptionally far-reaching aftereffects. In terms of institutional legacy, Emperor Wen ranks near the very front among the founding rulers of successive dynasties.

His later years took a different course: in Kaihuang 20 he deposed Crown Prince Yang Yong and installed the Prince of Jin, Yang Guang, in his place — and the struggle over the succession thereafter planted a risk to the dynasty's fortunes. In Renshou 4, Emperor Wen died at Renshou Palace; the historical record diverges on his dying orders and the succession process. His son [[sui-yang|Emperor Yang of Sui]] took the throne, and the legacy of unification was handed over together with the risk of a second-generation collapse — the controversy over the change of heir and the ending of "falling in the second generation" lowered later generations' appraisal of this founding. Read alongside [[qin-shi-huang|Qin Shi Huang]]: both completed reunification after long division, and both quickly plunged into crisis in the second generation — an unavoidable pair for anyone reading the history of unified empires.

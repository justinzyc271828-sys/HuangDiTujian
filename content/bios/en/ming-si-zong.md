# Zhu Youjian, Emperor Sizong of Ming (Chongzhen) · Major Deeds

Zhu Youjian (1611-1644), son of [[ming-guang-zong|Emperor Guangzong]] and younger brother of [[ming-xi-zong|Emperor Xizong]], took the throne in 1627, era Chongzhen.

For seventeen years he rose before dawn and worked past midnight - yet he was rigid and suspicious, replacing more than fifty chief secretaries and executing seven commanders. In 1630, deceived by a ruse, he executed Yuan Chonghuan and broke his own shield. Against the rebels he wavered between suppression and pardon while Li Zicheng and Zhang Xianzhong spread fire across the land. In March 1644 Li Zicheng took Beijing; the emperor climbed Coal Hill and hanged himself, leaving his body to the rebels but pleading for his people. The Ming fell.

The Qing then entered the passes and took the realm ([[qing-shi-zu|Emperor Shizu of Qing]]). A diligent emperor who died with his state - "no fallen sovereign ever ruled so frugally," the histories sigh.

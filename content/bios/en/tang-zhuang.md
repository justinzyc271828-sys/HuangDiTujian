# Emperor Zhuangzong of Later Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Cunxu, eldest son of the Shatuo Li Keyong, childhood name Yazi. In the 5th year of Tianyou (908) Keyong died and Cunxu succeeded as King of Jin, contending with Later Liang for over a decade — the will of the "three arrows" to wipe out Liang. In the 1st year of Tongguang (923) he took the throne at Weizhou as the realm Tang (Later Tang to history), claiming the legitimacy of the Tang house, and at once rode against Kaifeng with his own hand — Later Liang fell: "the will to destroy Liang, stretched out in a day." That year he moved the capital to Luoyang.

In the 3rd year of Tongguang he sent Guo Chongtao and the Prince of Wei to conquer Shu — Former Shu fell. Later Tang, holding both the Central Plains and Bashu, was the greatest of the Five Dynasties; but Zhuangzong was "bold and wanting in rites": he doted on actors (Jing Jin, Shi Yanqiong) and eunuchs, "making actors prefects," and the meritorious fell away; with Empress Liu he hoarded without limit.

In the 4th year of Tongguang the Weizhou garrison mutinied, and Li Siyuan (Mingzong) was carried south by his troops; Zhuangzong's own campaign failed, and in Luoyang the actor Guo Congqian and the others rose, Zhuangzong fell to an arrow and died, aged forty-two. The histories say: "bold and wanting in rites, favoring actors and eunuchs — he won, and did not last." He won the realm by arms and lost it by misrule — "decades of labor, ruined in a day" — the most typical rise and fall of the Five Dynasties.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 27–34 ("Annals of Emperor Zhuangzong").

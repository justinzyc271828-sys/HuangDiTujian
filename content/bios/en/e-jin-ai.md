# Emperor Ai of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Pi, styled Qianling, was the eldest son of Emperor Cheng by Lady Zhou, enfeoffed Prince of Langya. In the 5th year of Shengping Emperor Mu died without issue, and Pi, as Cheng's eldest son, took the throne at twenty, changing the era to Longhe. Old enough to govern in person, he chose instead to devote himself to Huang–Lao: he gave up grain and took the elixirs of immortality, and the business of government fell away.

In the 1st year of Longhe Huan Wen urged moving the capital to Luoyang; the court argued endlessly, the emperor pleaded "no leisure," and the matter died — but Wen's power had grown visibly. In the 1st year of Xingning Wen was made grand marshal with command over all the armies, more formidable than ever. In the 2nd year of Xingning the drugs overpowered the emperor; unable to attend to government, he gave the regency to Empress Chu again. In the second month of the 3rd year he died at twenty-five.

The histories record his death plainly: the drugs. His fasting and elixirs wasted him — the strange fate of an Eastern Jin sovereign. He was no tyrant and did nothing wrong; he simply destroyed himself chasing immortality. Huan Wen's power swelled in his reign, and the plot of deposition was already in view. Of the Eastern Jin emperors after Ming, most were children or weaklings; Ai, grown and healthy, was the more to be pitied for wasting himself.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 8 ("Benji of Emperor Ai"), ch. 98 (biography of Huan Wen).

# Wang Shenzhi, Founder of Min · Principal Deeds

Wang Shenzhi, a man of Gushi in Guangzhou, entered Min with his brother Wang Chao and Wang Xu; in mid-Qianning he succeeded his brother Chao as military governor of the Weiwu army, holding Fuzhou. In the 3rd year of Kaiping (909) Later Liang made him King of Min with his seat at Fuzhou — the beginning of Min. Frugal and courteous to the worthy, he founded schools, raised the Kaiyuan temple, and drew in the sea merchants — "the produce of Min reaching the ships," Fuzhou becoming the port of the southeast.

His government was light-levied: "he was frugal himself and rested the people," "drew the barbarian merchants and widened the sea trade"; he opened the Gantang harbor and the sea commerce flourished. Of the Ten Kingdoms, Min's letters and trade stood together at their height — the work of Shenzhi's ordering. He died in the 3rd year of Tongguang (925); the people of Min call him "the loyal and good."

The histories say: "a worthy founder; the people of Min call him loyal and good." But after his death his sons contended — Yanhan, Yanjun in turn, kin slaying kin, the government of Min falling into disorder until Southern Tang destroyed it in 945. Min rose on Shenzhi's benevolence and fell to his descendants' slaughter — "a worthy founder, unworthy heirs," the common fate of the Ten Kingdoms.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 68 ("Annals of Min").

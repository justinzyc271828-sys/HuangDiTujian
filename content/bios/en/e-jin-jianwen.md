# Emperor Jianwen of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Yu, styled Daowan, was the youngest son of Emperor Yuan, enfeoffed Prince of Kuajji. Pure and passionless by nature, a master of metaphysical talk, he was the patriarch of Eastern Jin's pure conversation; through the reigns of Cheng, Kang, Mu, Ai, and the deposed emperor he served as regent and grand secretary. In the 6th year of Taihe Huan Wen deposed the emperor and brought in Yu, changing the era to Xian'an — Yu was fifty-two, and reigned under a year.

His accession was wholly Huan Wen's doing: Wen came to court, and Yu, weeping, accepted the throne. Then Wen sought the nine gifts and looked toward abdication. The emperor, with Wang Tanzhi and Xie An, played for time; Wen fell ill, and the petition failed — "Wen's bid for the nine gifts came to nothing," and the Jin line was prolonged. In the seventh month of the 2nd year of Xian'an he died in the eastern hall at fifty-three, his will charging "the grand marshal Wen, after the manner of the Duke of Zhou's regency"; Emperor Xiaowu, eleven years old, succeeded.

The historians say of him: "Pure and passionless, a master of metaphysical talk." A conversationalist set on the imperial throne, he had neither the talent nor the will to rule; his single achievement was parrying Huan Wen's abdication, buying the Jin house a decade. That Xie An and Wang Tanzhi could play their game was thanks to their sovereign's humility. His reign had no great deeds; but by enduring the strong minister's grip without an inch of power, he kept the altars standing — no mean art in that age.

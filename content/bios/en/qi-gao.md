# Emperor Gao of Qi · Principal Deeds

Xiao Daocheng, styled Shaobo, was a man of Nanlanling, claiming descent from Xiao He of Han. He rose by merit under Song: putting down the Prince of Jingling's revolt and breaking the Prince of Guiyang, his name grew daily. In the 5th year of Yuanhui, when the Later Deposed Emperor moved to kill him, he struck first — his men slew the emperor and set up Emperor Shun; in the 2nd year of Shengming he destroyed Shen Youzhi of Jingzhou and held the government alone. In the 3rd year of Shengming, Emperor Shun abdicated: Daocheng took the throne, styled the realm Qi, and changed the era to Jianyuan — sixty years of Liu Song passed to the Xiao house in a day.

Four years he reigned, in frugality: "he would not use fine things," and said, "let me govern the realm ten years, and gold shall be worth no more than earth"; he fixed the rites and the offices and calmed the people; he revised the household registers, mending the abuses of the census. In the 4th year of Jianyuan he died at fifty-six; his heir Xiao Ze succeeded as Emperor Wu.

The histories say of him: "deep and of great measure, lenient and strict in due proportion." A common soldier who took the realm and kept it frugally — the rarest of the southern founders. But the Qi line was brief: his frugality did not outlast his sons; after Wu's Yongming recovery, the Princes of Yulin and Hailing were deposed in turn, Xiao Luan usurped, and within twenty years Qi was gone. Gao's virtue did not reach the third generation.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Nanqishu*, ch. 1 and 2 ("Benji of Emperor Gao").

# Wang Jian, Emperor Gaozu of Former Shu · Principal Deeds

Wang Jian, a man of Wuyang in Xuzhou, was a pie-seller in his youth, then a soldier of the Zhongwu army, "rising by merit." In mid-Dashun he attacked Chen Jingxuan, took Chengdu, and held the two Chuan; in mid-Tianfu Tang made him King of Shu. In the 7th year of Tianfu (907), when Zhu Wen usurped Tang, Wang Jian, "a minister of the Tang house, would not bow to Liang," took the imperial throne at Chengdu as the realm Shu — Former Shu began.

He was "bold and suspicious, good at handling his soldiers": he employed the old Tang ministers like Wei Zhuang, honored the worthy, and the culture of Shu was the finest of the Ten; he held examinations, and "the caps and gowns of Shu outshone the southwest." He died in the 1st year of Guangtian (918), aged seventy-two; his son Yan succeeded.

The histories say: "a bandit who rose to hold the lands of Shu." Former Shu stood on the shield of the two Chuan and the wealth of the heavenly prefecture, a strong state of the Ten; but Wang Jian's last years distrusted his meritorious men, and his heir Wang Yan was "a child at ease" — Former Shu fell in two generations, destroyed by Later Tang (925). "Swift to rise, swift to fall" — the fate of Former Shu is the Ten Kingdoms in miniature.

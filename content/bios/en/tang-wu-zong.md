# Emperor Wuzong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Yan, first named Chan, was the fifth son of Muzong. On Wenzong's death in the 5th year of Kaicheng the eunuch Qiu Shiliang and the others set up Yan, changing the era to Huichang. Wuzong made Li Deyu chancellor — the "Huichang revival" was Deyu's work: in the 3rd year he destroyed Liu Zhen of Zelu, and the feudatories lost heart for a moment; the Uyghur khanate fell, and he gathered its people; "Deyu's government was the best before Dazhong."

In the 5th year of Huichang Wuzong proscribed Buddhism by edict: over 4,600 monasteries demolished, over 260,000 monks and nuns returned to lay life — the Huichang persecution, paired with Emperor Taiwu of Northern Wei as the "two Wu persecutions." But he "loved Daoism and took the elixirs," and the drugs brought on his sickness and death at thirty-three, in the 6th year of Huichang.

The histories say: "with Li Deyu he won the name of the Huichang revival; he loved Daoism and the elixirs, and his years were not long." His reign was the strongest of Tang's arms and government since the An–Shi storm; but the persecution and the elixir-death made the revival a flash in the pan. The meeting of Wuzong's ability and Deyu's talent came too late and ended too soon — the chance of the restoration was gone.

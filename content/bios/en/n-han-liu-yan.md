# Liu Yan, Emperor Gaozu of Southern Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Yan, a man of Shangcai, succeeded his brother Liu Yin in mid-Qianhua as military governor of Qinghai, holding Lingnan; in the 1st year of Qianheng (917) he took the throne at Panyu as the realm Great Yue, soon renamed Han — Southern Han began. He "united Lingnan by merit": absorbing Yong and Rong in the west, soothing Jiaozhi in the south, all Lingnan passed to Southern Han.

But his "punishments and government were cruel beyond measure": the records tell of his tortures — "burning, boiling, flaying, excising, the knife-hill, the sword-tree," "mercury poured up the nose, iron beds to sear the flesh" — the cruelest of the Ten Kingdoms; he loved luxury, "jade halls and palaces of the utmost splendor," and in his last years doted on Buddhism, "the monks' cost ten thousand a day." He died in the 15th year of Dayou (942), aged fifty-four.

The histories say: "he united Lingnan by merit, but his punishments and government were poison." Southern Han stood on the rich lands of Lingnan; but Liu Yan's tyranny and his heirs' folly — the Shang, Zhongzong, and Last Sovereign — rotted the government, and Song destroyed it in 971. The fall of Southern Han was the fall of the Liu's cruelty — "the wealth of Lingnan could not save its cruelty."

# Murong Jun, Emperor Jingzhao of Former Yan · Principal Deeds

Murong Jun, styled Xuan Ying, was the second son of Murong Huang. On his father's death in the 4th year of Yonghe he succeeded as King of Yan. In the 6th year, riding the chaos after Shi Hu's death, he sent Murong Ke, Murong Ping, and the others south into Hebei, taking Ji and Zhongshan; in the 8th year he captured and slew Ran Min and took the imperial throne, styling the realm Yan — Former Yan entered the Central Plains, standing with Eastern Jin and Former Qin as the third of the age.

He moved the capital to Ye, the old seat of the Shi house, "his palaces and institutions modeled on Luoyang," and ordered the provinces to verify the household registers and encourage farming. At its height Former Yan "reached the Ru and Ying in the south, Qing and Qi in the east, and the Xiao and Mian in the west" — among the strongest of the Sixteen States. But in his later years he grew vainglorious: he would raise a million men against Jin and Qin to contest legitimacy, and he distrusted his brother Murong Chui. He died in the 4th year of Guangshou; his son Wei, eleven, succeeded under Murong Ke's regency.

For destroying Ran Wei and entering the Central Plains, Jun brought Former Yan to its height; but his endless wars and his suspicion of his own kin laid the fuse of its decline. The historians say he was "bold and full of stratagems," yet "a realm won by arms, not adorned by virtue — it rose swiftly and fell swiftly."

# Emperor Xiaojing of Eastern Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Shanjian was the son of the Prince of Qinghe, Yuan Dan. In the 3rd year of Yongxi, after Emperor Xiaowu fled west and was poisoned, Gao Huan received Shanjian as emperor and moved the capital to Ye — the beginning of Eastern Wei. Xiaojing was "sharp, fond of letters, and handsome," but Gao Huan held him: Huan sat at Jinyang ruling from afar, "rewards and punishments all coming from Huan," and the emperor folded his hands. Sixteen years Eastern and Western Wei stood side by side, the great battles of Shayuan, Mangshan, and Yubi fought to a standstill — Huan could never destroy the west.

In the 5th year of Wuding Huan died, and his son Gao Cheng took the government, pressing the emperor harder; Xiaojing once cried out, "Of what use is my life!" In the 8th year of Wuding, Gao Yang (Cheng's brother) received the abdication — Eastern Wei was no more — and Xiaojing, made Prince of Zhongshan, was soon done to death, aged twenty-eight.

Sixteen years the only sovereign of Eastern Wei: "a puppet, yet keeping the sovereign's bearing" — he played Go with Gao Cheng without changing color. But "sharp and fond of letters, in the end the Gao house's captive." The fall of Eastern Wei marked the Gao house's succession — the rise of Northern Qi ran through the puppet throne of Eastern Wei. The historians absolve Shanjian: "a child receiving the abdication, watching the realm pass, dying with the state — it was not his fault."

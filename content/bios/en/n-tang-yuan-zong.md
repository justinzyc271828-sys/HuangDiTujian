# Li Jing, Yuan Zong of Southern Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Jing, eldest son of Li Bian, took the throne in the 1st year of Baoda (943). The Yuan Zong was "of high bearing, a lover of letters," at ease with the ci poets Feng Yansi, Feng Yanlu, Han Xizai, and the rest — "stirring a spring pond's water" passing into the talk of the age: the glory of Southern Tang ci began with him.

But he was "fond of great designs": in mid-Baoda, riding Min's internal wars, he sent armies to destroy part of Min, and contended with Chu — the borders widened while "the wars dragged on and the state's purse shrank." In the 1st year of Jiaotai (958) Emperor Shizong of Zhou campaigned south; Southern Tang was shattered, Jing dropped the imperial title for "ruler of the south of the river," and ceded the fourteen prefectures north of the Yangzi — the turn of Southern Tang from strength to decline. In the 2nd year of Jianlong (961) he moved the capital to Hongzhou and died, aged forty-six; his son Yu (Li Yu) succeeded.

The histories say: "he inherited and held, loving letters; he widened the borders and lost by misjudgment." The decline of Southern Tang began with his widening: the north of the river lost, the state's strength ebbed — and Li Yu, inheriting, "forty years of home and realm," fell to Song at last. The Yuan Zong's ci and the Yuan Zong's loss are the two faces of Southern Tang.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 62 ("Annals of Southern Tang"), and the *Nantangshu*.

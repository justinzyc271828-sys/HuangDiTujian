# Emperor Xiaomin of Northern Zhou · Principal Deeds

Yuwen Jue was the third son of Yuwen Tai. After Tai's death his nephew Yuwen Hu took the regency; in the 3rd year of the Gong emperor of Western Wei, Hu pressed the emperor to abdicate to Jue, who took the title Heavenly King of the realm Zhou — the beginning of Northern Zhou. Xiaomin was fifteen, and bore the title of king, not yet emperor (the rite of the *Zhouli*, per Tai's design).

He would not be Hu's puppet: with Li Zhi, Sun Heng, and the others he plotted against Hu, and the plot leaked. That same ninth month Hu deposed him as Duke of Lüeyang, confined him in the old mansion, and soon slew him, aged sixteen. His enthronement, his deposition, and his death all fell within a year — the founder of Northern Zhou slain by his minister.

The histories say: "a child receiving the abdication, the power with the Duke of Jin, Hu." Northern Zhou ran on the *Zhouli* — the title of Heavenly King, the six ministries, all Tai's design; but the throne was not yet strong when the minister was, and Xiaomin was but a passing figure. The throne of Zhou recovered only when Emperor Wu slew Hu — Xiaomin's death was the first eruption of the minister's tyranny in Northern Zhou.

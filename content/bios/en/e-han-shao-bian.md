# The Young Emperor Bian · Principal Deeds

Liu Bian was the son of [[e-han-ling|Emperor Ling]] by Empress He. When Ling died in the sixth Zhongping year, Bian took the throne with Empress Dowager He in the regency and General-in-Chief He Jin in power. The last court drama of Eastern Han was played out in these few months.

He Jin meant to destroy the eunuchs, but hesitated and instead summoned troops from the provinces to overawe the court — including Dong Zhuo, governor of Bingzhou. The plot leaked; the eunuchs led by Zhang Rang struck first and slew He Jin; Yuan Shao and others stormed the palace and slaughtered more than two thousand eunuchs. The "chaos of the Ten Attendants" ended with eunuchs and consorts destroying each other. Dong Zhuo, arriving on cue, was the sole victor: in the ninth month he deposed Bian as Prince of Hongnong and raised the prince of Chenliu, Xie, as [[e-han-xian|Emperor Xian]].

Bian had reigned under half a year. Deposed, he was shut up in a tower; in the first month of the first Chuping year Dong Zhuo sent Li Ru with poisoned wine. Bian sang a farewell to his wife Tang Ji as he drank, and died at fourteen. He never made a single decision of his own, yet he witnessed the final collapse of Han sovereignty: before him the emperor had been the prize fought over by consorts and eunuchs; after him, the emperor became a seal in the hands of warlords.

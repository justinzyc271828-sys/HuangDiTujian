# Emperor Xuanwu of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Ke, second son of Emperor Xiaowen, took the throne at seventeen when Xiaowen died on the southern campaign in the 23rd year of Taihe, changing the era to Jingming. In the 1st year of Jingming he dismissed the six regents and took up the government himself. His sixteen years turned from Xiaowen's eager reform to stewardship. His age faced Emperor Wu of Liang: in mid-Jingming he contended for Huainan and Yiyang with Liang, fortunes divided — until the great defeat at Zhongli, and the southern advance of Wei was checked.

His age saw Buddhism at its height: the princes and nobles raced to raise monasteries, "the monasteries of Luoyang packed like the teeth of a comb," over a thousand of them; the emperor lectured on the sutras himself and ordained monks. But the monastery economy grew, and monks freed from taxes — one seed of the realm's later ills. He carried on the sinicization, but "governed loosely, the net of the law wide," and the princes grew arrogant; the diseases of Northern Wei gathered.

He died in the 4th year of Yanchang at thirty-three; his son Xu, Emperor Xiaoming, succeeded, with Empress Hu entering the government — the turn of Northern Wei from height to decline. The histories say he "truly loved letters and, once on the throne, gave real care to government," yet "trusted the petty men, and the court's discipline slackened." He inherited Xiaowen's enterprise but not his purpose; the decline of Wei began in his reign.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 8 ("Benji of Emperor Xuanwu"), ch. 114 ("Treatise on Buddhism and Daoism").

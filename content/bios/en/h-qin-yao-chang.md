# Yao Chang, Founder of Later Qin · Principal Deeds

Yao Chang, styled Jingmao, was a Qiang of Chiting in Nan'an, twenty-fourth son of Yao Yizhong. After the Fei River defeat, as Former Qin crumbled, Chang turned against Fu Jian in the 1st year of Baique and styled himself King of Wannian Qin in Beidi — the beginning of Later Qin. In the 2nd year of Baique he besieged Xinping; Fu Jian, fled to Mount Wujiang, was seized and strangled — the great hero of Former Qin died by his own minister's hand.

In the 1st year of Jianchu Chang took the imperial throne at Chang'an, styling the realm Great Qin. The founding years were hard: Fu Deng held the Longdong with the remnants of Former Qin, and Chang fought him between Anding and Xinping for years without decision; the commanderies of Guanzhong rose and were put down. In the 8th year of Jianchu, gravely ill with Fu Deng pressing, he wished to flee; his men would not let him, and he died at Chang'an, posthumously Emperor Wuzhao.

A Qiang who rose with the times, slew Fu Jian, and founded a realm — the historians call him an adventurer; his slaying of his lord and his jealousy blacken his name among the Sixteen States' sovereigns. Later Qin rose by Chang's cunning and lost its virtue by his crime; his son Yao Xing's patronage of learning and Buddhism partly repaired the name, but could not wash it clean.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 116 (records of Yao Chang), ch. 114 (records of Fu Jian).

# The Prince of Yulin · Principal Deeds

Xiao Zhaoye, styled Yuanshang, was the eldest son of the Crown Prince Wenhui and Emperor Wu's grandson. On Wu's death in the 11th year of Yongming he took the throne at twenty-one, changing the era to Longchang. The Prince of Yulin was "frivolous and worthless": the records tell of his "nightlong play with the palace boys," his gambling, his "money games," while the government fell to the Marquis of Xichang, Xiao Luan (Emperor Ming).

Within a year of his accession Luan, governing in his name, gathered the power: first he destroyed the party of the Prince of Jingling, then the old ministers of Emperor Gao; in the seventh month of the 1st year of Longchang, by the empress dowager's edict, he deposed Zhaoye as Prince of Yulin and soon had him slain, aged twenty-two. His deposition opened Qi's "child sovereign, doubtful state": with the sons of Gao and Wu still living, a senior prince usurped — the family wars of the southern courts replaying in Qi.

The histories say of him: "handsome in his youth, a master of clerical script," yet "artful and dissolute, reckless in pleasure." The grandson of Wu, the heir of Wenhui, he should have carried on the Yongming recovery; but "untaught in childhood, ungoverned in youth," he was seized by Luan. His fall was the hinge of the Qi line: after it, Luan slaughtered the sons of Gao and Wu, the princes' strength was spent, and the Marquis of Donghun's ruin and Xiao Yan's succession were but its aftermath.

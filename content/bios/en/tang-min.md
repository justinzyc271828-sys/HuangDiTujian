# Emperor Min of Later Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Conghou, third son of Mingzong, took the throne on his father's death in the 4th year of Changxing at twenty, changing the era to Yingshun. The Min emperor was "young and weak," and on his accession the princes — the Prince of Lu, Congke, and the rest — and the feudatories held him in contempt: Zhu Hongzhao and Feng Yun, governing for him, plotted to strip the Prince of Lu of his garrisons — the transfer that provoked the storm.

In the 1st year of Yingshun (934) the Fengxiang governor Li Congke rose, denouncing Zhu Hongzhao and the rest; the emperor's armies went over to him, and Congke rode into Luoyang. The Min emperor fled, was seized, deposed as Prince of E, and soon done to death, aged twenty-one — four months on the throne.

The histories say: "young and weak, unable to control the feudatories and the princes." In the Five Dynasties, "whoever is strong in soldiers and horses is the Son of Heaven": a bookish sovereign in the warlord age, his deposition and death were the logic of the age. The fall of Later Tang quickened from his deposition — Congke, ill-gotten on the throne, burned himself within two years.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 45 ("Annals of the Min Emperor").

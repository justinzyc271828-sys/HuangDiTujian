# Emperor Jing of Liang · Principal Deeds

Xiao Fangzhi, ninth son of Emperor Yuan, was Prince of Jin'an when Jiangling fell in the 3rd year of Chengsheng and Emperor Yuan was slain. Brought to Jiankang, he took the throne in the 1st year of Shaotai at thirteen. Liang was now a name only: Northern Qi propped up the Marquis of Zhenyang, Xiao Yuanming, and Wang Sengbian for a time received him; Chen Baxian struck down Sengbian and restored Fangzhi — the power of Jiankang was already Baxian's.

Three years he reigned: in the 1st year of Taiping Baxian held the Qi armies at Jiankang and Jiangdong was roughly settled; in the tenth month of the 2nd year of Taiping Baxian received the abdication — Liang was no more. Fangzhi, enfeoffed Prince of Jiangyin, was done to death by Baxian's men the next year (the 2nd of Yongding), aged sixteen. From Emperor Wu's founding, Liang ran five sovereigns and fifty-six years.

"Caught in the storm as a child, caught in the abdication": his accession, his abdication, and his death were all beyond his choosing. After Liang fell, the southern stage passed to Chen Baxian — and Chen lasted but thirty-three years, the briefest of the southern dynasties. The historians absolve Fangzhi: a thirteen-year-old watching his grandfather's realm pass was not the cause; Liang fell to the Hou Jing rebellion and the family slaughter, the disease long planted.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Liangshu*, ch. 6 ("Benji of Emperor Jing"), and the *Chenshu*, ch. 1 ("Benji of Emperor Gao").

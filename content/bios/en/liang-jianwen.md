# Emperor Jianwen of Liang · Principal Deeds

Xiao Gang, styled Shizuan, was the third son of Emperor Wu, younger brother of the Crown Prince Zhaoming. Born in the 2nd year of Tianjian, he was sharp from infancy, writing prose at six. Enfeoffed Prince of Jin'an, he served as governor and formed a circle with Xu Chi and Yu Jianwu, championing the "palace style" — a poetry of court life and feminine grace, ornate and sensuous, one of the great schools of southern literature. In the 3rd year of Taiqing, Hou Jing took the palace; Emperor Wu starved to death, and Gang took the throne, changing the era to Dabao.

Two years he reigned as Jing's puppet: Jing made himself chancellor and "Grand General of the Universe," deciding everything while the emperor folded his hands. In the 2nd year of Dabao Jing degraded him as Prince of Jin'an, confined him in the Yongfu Hall, and soon had him slain, aged forty-nine. To the end he wrote poems of his own sorrow; the *Nanshi* records his cry, "I have read ten thousand books, and yet this day."

A man of letters on a dying throne: his palace style opened the sensuous poetry of the Qi–Liang age, the herald of Tang verse; but "letters in plenty, and in the storm he could not rise" — he watched the altars fall without the power to move. The Hou Jing rebellion was the hinge of the southern decline, and Jianwen was but its victim: the age of "four hundred eighty temples in the southern land" was at an end.

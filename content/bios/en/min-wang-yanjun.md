# Wang Yanjun, Emperor Huizong of Min · Principal Deeds

Wang Yanjun, second son of Wang Shenzhi, succeeded in the 1st year of Tiancheng (926) after his brother Yanhan was slain; in mid-Tiancheng he took the name Wang Lin. In the 1st year of Longqi (933) he took the imperial title, the realm of Min — Min first bore the emperor's name. But he "loved the immortals and raised great works": he built the Baohuang Palace, made monks of the people, and trusted the Daoist Chen Shouyuan, "the government decided by shamans and prayers."

In the 1st year of Yonghe (935) his son Wang Chang, with the palace commander Li Fang and the others, slew him in the palace, around forty. About nine years he reigned, and the government of Min passed from Shenzhi's ordering into the sons' wars.

The histories say: "he took the imperial title, and the government fell into disorder." From Yanjun's title on, the throne of Min passed by kin slaying kin: Yanjun slew his brother to rise, and his son Chang slew him — "the disorders of Min began with Yanjun." Wang Chang, Wang Yanxi, Wang Yanzheng followed, and Min, torn within, was destroyed by Southern Tang in 945.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 68 ("Annals of Min").

# Yang Xingmi, Founder of Wu · Principal Deeds

Yang Xingmi, a man of Hefei in Luzhou, rose in the late-Tang chaos from the "eight camps of Luzhou"; in mid-Jingfu he became military governor of Huainan with his seat at Yangzhou — the foundation of the Yang Wu realm. In mid-Qianning he contended with Sun Ru for the Jiang-Huai, destroyed him, and fixed the Huainan: "soldiers fine and grain ample, the finest of the southeast." He drew in the worthy — Yuan Xi, Gao Xun, and the rest — kept his borders, and rested his people; farming flourished and the markets were quiet, a modest peace in the storm.

In the 2nd year of Tianfu Tang made him King of Wu. Over a decade he nourished his officers and loved his people, mending the waterways and urging the plough, "the people of Huainan gaining rest." But after his death his sons Wo and Longyan in turn could not check Xu Wen — the power of the Yang house passed to the Xu.

He died in the 2nd year of Tianyou, aged fifty-four. The histories say: "he kept his borders and rested his people — the Jiang-Huai knew modest peace." Of the Ten Kingdoms, Yang Wu was broad and strong, the great state of the south; and Xingmi's rule, fixing the Jiang-Huai, opened the two generations of Yang Wu and Southern Tang.

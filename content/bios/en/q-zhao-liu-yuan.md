# Liu Yuan, Founder of Han (Former Zhao) · Principal Deeds

Liu Yuan, styled Yuanhai, was a Xiongnu of Xinxing, descended from Modu. Studious from youth, he mastered the Mao Odes, the Jing-school Changes, and the military art of Sun and Wu, "long of arm, a fine archer, of unusual strength." When the War of the Eight Princes broke out, he rose at Lishi, styled himself Great Chanyu, then King of Han — claiming descent through a Han princess, he honored Liu Shan as Emperor Xiaohuai and set up the ancestral tablets of the Han emperors to contest legitimacy with the Jin. The Sixteen States era begins with Liu Yuan.

In the 2nd year of Yongjia he took the imperial throne, styled the realm Han, moved his capital to Pingyang, and installed his officials; his lieutenants Liu Cong and Wang Mi attacked Luoyang without success — the prelude to its fall. But the Xiongnu Han state ran on a divided system of Hu and Han, arms and government mixed, and its foundations were never firm. In the 4th year of Yongjia Liu Yuan died, posthumously Guangwen. His son He succeeded and was at once slain by his brother Cong, and the state passed to Cong.

A Xiongnu who rode the Jin collapse to found a state, Liu Yuan opened the "Five Barbarian invasions." His claim of succession to Han and his adoption of Han rites show how deep Chinese culture had sunk into him; yet his state stood on arms and passed on tyranny — heirs murdering heirs, the people's hearts lost — and perished within a generation. His rising was ambition, not benevolence; the historians, beyond his "quickness and love of learning," see the beginning of the Hu–Han conflict.

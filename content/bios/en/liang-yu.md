# Xiao Dong, Prince of Yuzhang · Principal Deeds

Xiao Dong was the grandson of the Crown Prince Zhaoming, son of Xiao Huan, Prince of Yuzhang. In the 2nd year of Dabao, Hou Jing deposed Emperor Jianwen and set up Dong, changing the era to Tianzheng. Dong was young and weak, with no bearing of a sovereign; once on the throne, the records say, "he ate with his wife and wrote on paper," while Jing played with him as with a child — his months of reign were decided wholly by Jing.

In the 1st year of Tianzheng Jing forced Dong to abdicate and took the throne himself as Emperor of Han, reducing Dong to Prince of Huaiyin, confined in the Yongfu Hall. In the 1st year of Chengsheng, Wang Sengbian and Chen Baxian destroyed Hou Jing; the brothers Dong were released and thought themselves saved — but Emperor Yuan of Liang had them seized as "remnants of Hou Jing's party" and slain, none of them past twenty, as the *Nanshi* records their last hours with pity.

Dong's enthronement and his fall were both Hou Jing's toys: the grandson of Zhaoming in the Liang imperial sequence, in fact the extreme of the age's puppetry. The gazetteer keeps him in the Liang line as quasi-emperor. The historians say: "young, weak, powerless — not his own master." In the Hou Jing storm none of the Liang princes escaped; Dong was only the most innocent of them.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Liangshu*, ch. 56 (biography of Hou Jing), and the *Nanshi*, ch. 53 (the sons of Zhaoming).

# The Deposed Emperor of Chen · Principal Deeds

Chen Bozong, eldest son of Emperor Wen, took the throne on his father's death in the 1st year of Tiankang at thirteen, changing the era to Guangda. The Deposed Emperor was "young and weak, the government his uncle's": the Prince of Ancheng, Chen Xu (Emperor Xuan), his father's younger brother, governed as regent and grand secretary, deciding everything; in the 1st year of Guangda he slew the regents Liu Shizhi and Dao Zhongju, and the whole government passed to one man.

In the 2nd year of Guangda, Xu, by the empress dowager's edict, deposed Bozong as Prince of Linhai and took the throne himself, changing the era to Taijian — Chen's practice of brother succeeding brother began here. The deposed emperor lived at Linhai and died in the 2nd year of Taijian at seventeen, some say by Xuan's hand.

The historians say: "young and weak, the power with the Prince of Ancheng." A thirteen-year-old before a strong uncle, his fate was decided for him. The family wars of the southern courts ran on into Chen: Xuan won the throne by deposing his nephew, and his son the Last Sovereign lost the realm within twenty years. The Deposed Emperor's fate matches the Young Emperor of Song and the Princes of Yulin and Hailing of Qi — "a child sovereign, a doubtful state," the old disease of the south.

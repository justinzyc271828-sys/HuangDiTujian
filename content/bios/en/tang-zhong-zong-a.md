# Emperor Zhongzong of Tang (First Reign) · Principal Deeds

Li Xian, later renamed Zhe, was the seventh son of Gaozong by Empress Wu. On Gaozong's death in the 1st year of Hongdao he took the throne, changing the era to Sisheng — his reign lasted fifty-five days. Seeking to make his wife's father Wei Xuanzhen chamberlain, he was deposed by Wu as Prince of Luling and moved to Fangzhou, Wu declaring "the realm belongs to the Wu house."

In exile at Fangzhou he "tasted every peril," and "at every edict-bearer's coming he would start and think of suicide," until his wife Wei comforted him: "fortune and misfortune lean on each other — what is constant?" In the 1st year of Shengli, in the matter of the succession, Wu recalled him and made him crown prince again — the return from Fangzhou, the prelude to the restoration. Through the Wu Zhou years he sat at the eastern capital as heir, in constant fear.

A month on the throne and deposed, the histories say: "a month in office, the power with Empress Wu." Wu, a mother holding court and deposing emperors, opened the way to the only female sovereign of China; and Zhongzong's deposition was the transition to the Zhou. His restoration is recorded under "Emperor Zhongzong of Tang (Restored)."

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 7 ("Benji of Emperor Zhongzong"), ch. 6 ("Benji of Empress Zetian").

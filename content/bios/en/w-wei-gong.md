# Emperor Gong of Western Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Kuo, fourth son of Emperor Wen, was set up by Yuwen Tai after the Deposed Emperor's fall in the 3rd year. Two years and more he reigned, the government wholly Tai's: Tai ran the state on the *Rites of Zhou*, restoring the six ministries of antiquity — the government of Western Wei was Tai's government. In the 3rd year Yuwen Tai died at fifty; his nephew Yuwen Hu took the regency, and Tai's son Yuwen Jue inherited the power.

That same year Hu pressed Gong to abdicate to Jue — Western Wei was no more, and Northern Zhou arose. Gong, made Duke of Song, was soon done to death. Western Wei, three emperors and twenty-three years from Wen's accession, passed away; but its institutional legacy — the fubing system, the six ministries, the Guanzhong bloc — passed entire to Northern Zhou and thence to Sui and Tang.

The histories say of him: "caught in the abdication, he ended Western Wei." A child sovereign watching the Yuwens take the realm, not his own master; his abdication and death mirror Emperor Xiaojing of Eastern Wei — the two Wei, both transitional states of strong ministers. Western Wei died, but the Guanzhong-based institutions and the fubing system it created were the direct source of the Sui–Tang empire.

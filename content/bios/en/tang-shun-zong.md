# Emperor Shunzong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Song, eldest son of Dezong, took the throne in the first month of the 21st year of Zhenyuan, changing the era to Yongzhen. Already struck by paralysis and unable to speak, he yet meant to clear the abuses: with his eastern-palace men Wang Shuwen and Wang Pi, joined by Liu Zongyuan, Liu Yuxi, Wei Zhiyi, and the rest, he plotted to take the eunuchs' armies, abolish the market-depredations and the five-teams kennels, and return the tribute — the "Yongzhen reform."

But the eunuch Ju Wenzhen and the rest, linking with the feudatories, pressed the heir Chun (Xianzong) to demand the abdication: in the eighth month of the 1st year of Yongzhen Shunzong yielded to the heir and styled himself Grand Emperor — the reform died after a hundred and forty-six days; Wang Shuwen was ordered to die, Wang Pi died in exile, and the eight others, Liu Zongyuan and the rest, were banished as prefectural marshals — the "Two Kings and Eight Marshals." In the first month of the 1st year of Yuanhe the Grand Emperor died, aged forty-six.

The histories say: "his reign was brief; he meant to clear the abuses and could not finish." A sick man on the throne, his will to reform and his choice of men might have restored the Tang; but the eunuchs' strength and the depth of the old abuses were beyond one reign. The reform failed, yet its program — check the eunuchs, abolish the market-depredations — was the forerunner of Wenzong's Ganlu attempt: the eunuch domination of Tang was by then unbreakable.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 14 ("Benji of Emperor Shunzong"), and Han Yu's *Shunzong Shilu*.

# Emperor Gong of Sui · Principal Deeds

Yang You, son of the Crown Prince Zhao, was a grandson of Emperor Yang. In the 13th year of Daye, Li Yuan, risen at Taiyuan and marching west, took Chang'an and set up You, changing the era to Yining and honoring Emperor Yang at Jiangdu as Grand Emperor from afar — the real power of Sui was already Li Yuan's. At the eastern capital, the Prince of Yue, Yang Tong, was in turn set up by Wang Shichong and the others as the "Huangtai Sovereign": two emperors of Sui at once, both toys of strong ministers.

In the third month of the 2nd year of Yining, Emperor Yang was slain at Jiangdu by Yuwen Huaji; in the fifth month Li Yuan received the abdication and founded Tang, and You, made Duke of Fu, stepped down. In the 2nd year of Wude he died, aged fifteen — Sui, thirty-eight years from Wen's founding, was no more.

His enthronement, his abdication, and his death were none of his choosing: a child on the throne in the great chaos, the instrument of Li Yuan's "holding the emperor to command the lords." The historians say Sui fell to Emperor Yang's tyranny and his wars; Gong only inherited the disaster. The gazetteer keeps him in the Sui line as quasi-emperor. Sui fell and Tang rose, and the three hundred years of the Tang opened.

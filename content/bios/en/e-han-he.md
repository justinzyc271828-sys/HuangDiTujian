# Emperor He of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Zhao was the son of [[e-han-zhang|Emperor Zhang of Han]]. He took the throne in the second Zhanghe year at ten, with Empress Dowager Dou in the regency and Dou Xian and his brothers in power. This was the beginning of Eastern Han's child-emperor politics: from then on, consort regencies, eunuch meddling, and infant sovereigns formed the three-part theme of the dynasty's middle and late periods.

Two great matters mark his reign. The first was military: in the first Yongyuan year Dou Xian broke the northern Xiongnu and carved a commemoration on Mount Yanran, for which Ban Gu composed the "Yanran Mountain Inscription"; in the third Yongyuan year the northern chanyu fled far away, and the northern Xiongnu began their westward migration, fading from the Chinese records — the last great scene of the Han-Xiongnu wars, commanded by an ambitious consort. The second was the recovery of power: in the fourth Yongyuan year the fourteen-year-old emperor plotted with the eunuch Zheng Zhong, seized Dou Xian's seal of office, and the Dou brothers all died; the consort scourge was briefly laid. But the price came at once: Zheng Zhong was enfeoffed as Marquis of Chaoxiang, and the rise of enfeoffed, powerful eunuchs began here.

After taking power he followed the Ming-Zhang precedents: in the ninth Yongyuan year locusts struck, and he summoned the worthy and sought counsel for the people; Ban Chao's work in the Western Regions culminated in the sixth Yongyuan year with the submission of Yanqi, the states of the west sending hostages to court, and in the fourteenth Yongyuan year the aging Ban Chao returned to Luoyang, dying the next year. In the first Yuanxing year Emperor He died at twenty-seven. The historians say the Yongyuan government "followed the established precedents without lapse," yet in an age of child emperors the consorts and eunuchs had already begun their alternation — the fourteen years of He's personal rule were the transition zone of Eastern Han's decline.

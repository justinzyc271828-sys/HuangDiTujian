# Zhang Gui, Martial King of Former Liang · Principal Deeds

Zhang Gui, styled Shiyan, was a man of Wushi in Anding, "sharp and studious, of high repute." In the 1st year of Yongning, with the War of the Eight Princes blazing, he asked for the governorship of Liangzhou and held Hexi. While the Central Plains collapsed he kept his borders and his people, encouraged farming and mulberries, and founded schools — "Liangzhou, since Gui, was called the land of delight" — and the gentry and refugees of the Jin fled west to him.

His rule: he broke the Xianbei Ruoluo Baneng and his name ran through Hexi; with Zhou Qi and the others as counselors he furthered letters and established academies; "loyal to the Jin house," he never ceased tribute and received Jin's titles. He died of illness in the 2nd year of Jianxing, posthumously Duke Wu, and his son Shi succeeded. The Zhang house held Liangzhou through nine rulers and seventy-six years (301–376), the longest-lived of the Sixteen States — though it never took the imperial title, its self-ruling Hexi was a true power.

The historians say of him: "he cherished the Qiang and the Hu, and carried civilization through the river lands." While the Central Plains went under, Liangzhou alone kept its peace; besides the caps and gowns crossing south, there was a westward flight of scholars — "Liangzhou's cattle were the realm's wealth" — and Gui's rule was a refuge in the storm. His descendants were later swept away by Former Qin, but the virtue of Former Liang was long remembered.

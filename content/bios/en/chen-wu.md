# Emperor Wu of Chen · Principal Deeds

Chen Baxian, styled Xingguo, was a man of Changcheng in Wuxing, of common birth — "unconventional from youth and great of purpose" — who began as a village head and rose by merit of arms to be Governor of Jiaozhou. When the Hou Jing rebellion broke, he rose from Guangzhou and joined Wang Sengbian against Jing, entering Jiankang in the 3rd year of Taiqing to destroy the rebel: the "restoration of the Liang house" was chiefly the work of Baxian and Sengbian.

In the 1st year of Shaotai, when Sengbian received the Marquis of Zhenyang propped up by Qi, Baxian opposed it, struck Sengbian down at Stone City, restored the Prince of Jin'an, and took the government himself; in the 1st year of Taiping he drove the Qi armies back at Jiankang, and Jiangdong was saved. In the 1st year of Yongding Emperor Jing abdicated; Baxian took the throne, styled the realm Chen, and changed the era — of the four southern dynasties, Chen's years were the fewest and its founding the hardest.

Three years he reigned, dying in the 3rd year of Yongding at fifty-seven. Chen stood on ground laid waste by the Hou Jing storm — "the south of the river in ruins" — and Baxian, a common man, took the realm from chaos; but his reign was brief and the succession unsecured, until his nephew Chen Qian (Emperor Wen) carried on. The histories say of him: "of heroic strategy and open measure" — the pillar of the southern courts' last age for two deeds, the destruction of Hou Jing and the holding of the Qi.

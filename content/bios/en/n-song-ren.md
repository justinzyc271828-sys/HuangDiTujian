# Zhao Zhen, Emperor Renzong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Zhen, sixth son of [[n-song-zhen|Emperor Zhenzong]], ascended at thirteen in 1022 under the regency of Empress Liu, who held power until her death in 1033.

His forty-two-year reign was the cultural zenith of Northern Song: Fan Zhongyan, Ouyang Xiu, Bao Zheng, and Su Shi rose together; censors flourished and plain speech became the fashion. In 1043 Fan Zhongyan and Fu Bi proposed the "Qingli Reforms," abandoned within little more than a year under entrenched opposition. In 1044 peace was made with [[xixia-li-yuanhao|Yuanhao of Western Xia]], who submitted as a vassal in return for annual gifts.

Renzong was famously tolerant and benevolent - it was said that even market songs reached his ears - and his later years are praised as the "Jiayou governance." Yet drift accumulated: redundant officials and soldiers, lavish expense, and weakening borders planted the seeds of Wang Anshi's reforms.

He died in 1063 without a surviving son; Zhao Shu, son of Prince Pu Anyi, was made heir and became [[n-song-ying|Emperor Yingzong]].

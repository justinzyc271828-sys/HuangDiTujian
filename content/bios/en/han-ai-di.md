# Emperor Ai of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Xin, the adopted heir of [[han-cheng-di|Emperor Cheng]], had been king of Dingtao; he entered the succession in the first Suihe year and took the throne when Cheng died the next year. Young and eager to act, he meant from the start to break the grip of the Wang consorts on the government — yet in six years every reform came to nothing, and the Han mandate slid faster toward Wang Mang.

Each great matter of his reign aimed at "strengthening the sovereign" and each failed: on accession he ordered a debate on limiting fields and slaves; Shi Dan's proposals died in the face of aristocratic opposition. In the second Jianping year he let Xia Heliang and others persuade him to change era and title, producing the farce of "the Chengsheng Liu Taiping emperor," soon rescinded. To counter the Wangs he raised his grandmother's Fu clan, his mother's Ding clan, and above all Dong Xian — whose favor exceeded all precedent, the "severed sleeve" infatuation passing into proverb, while the throne's authority only weakened.

In the second Yuanshou year the Wuzhuliu chanyu came to court, one of the last dignified scenes of four-tribes homage in Western Han; in the sixth month of that year Ai died without issue. The grand empress dowager Wang Zhengjun took back the seal the same day, summoned [[xin-wang-mang|Wang Mang]] into the palace, and Dong Xian killed himself. The historians' eulogy: "Having seen in the reign of Cheng the offices drained from the royal house, he wished to strengthen the sovereign's authority — but the Dings, the Fus, and Dong Xian disordered it." He was not without the will to reform; the institutional disease of Western Han was simply past cure by any single ruler.

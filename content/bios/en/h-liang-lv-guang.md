# Lü Guang, Martial Emperor of Later Liang · Principal Deeds

Lü Guang, styled Shiming, was a Di of Lüeyang and a general of Fu Jian. In the 19th year of Jianyuan, by Fu Jian's order, he campaigned in the Western Regions: with seventy thousand horse and foot he crossed the drifting sands, broke Karashahr and Kucha, "the kingdoms all submitting," and brought back Kumarajiva. In the 1st year of Tai'an, returning east on word that Yao Chang had slain Fu Jian, he held Guzang and stood alone, styling himself Governor of Liangzhou; in the 1st year of Longfei he took the title King of the Three Rivers, later Heavenly King — the beginning of Later Liang.

Guanzheng he was strong; governing he was without art: he made generals of his sons, and the sons fought; he distrusted his kin and slew them. In his old age Tufa Wugu, Juqu Mengxun, Duan Ye, and the others rose against him one after another, and his realm was torn into four. He died in the 1st year of Chengkang; his sons contended, Later Liang decayed, and within years Later Qin took it.

The historians say he was "resolute from youth and of real bearing," yet "jealous by nature, untrusting of his subordinates." With the glory of the western campaign and the possession of Liangzhou he might have built a lasting state; but he ruled as a soldier, without the arts of conciliation, the peoples of Hexi fell away, and Later Liang passed swiftly. His western conquest stands alone among the Sixteen States' sovereigns; but the glory of arms did not cure the failure of government.

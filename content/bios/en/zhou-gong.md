# Emperor Gong of Later Zhou · Principal Deeds

Chai Zongxun, fourth son of Emperor Shizong, took the throne on his father's death in the 6th year of Xiande (959) at seven. The Gong emperor was a child; the government fell to the chancellors Fan Zhi and Wang Pu, and to the commander of the palace guard, Zhao Kuangyin.

In the first month of the 7th year of Xiande (960), on word of a Liao–Northern Han invasion (perhaps a false alarm), Zhao Kuangyin led the imperial guard north against it; at the Chenqiao post station the army mutinied and robed him in yellow — the Chenqiao mutiny, the last mutiny of the Five Dynasties. Kuangyin returned to Kaifeng and forced the Gong emperor's abdication, founding Song and changing the era to Jianlong — Later Zhou was no more, the Five Dynasties ended, and Northern Song began.

The former emperor, made Prince of Zheng, was moved to Fangzhou and died in the 6th year of Kaibao (973), aged twenty-one (some say by Song's hand). The histories say: "a child in the abdication — the Five Dynasties ended." In the fifty-three years of the Five Dynasties, eight surnames and fourteen emperors, "whoever is strong in soldiers and horses is the Son of Heaven" — the Chenqiao mutiny was the last of the age's usurpations by mutiny, and the direct lesson behind the Song founder's "letting go of military power over wine." With Song taking Zhou's place, Chinese history entered the two Song dynasties.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 120 ("Annals of the Zhou Gong Emperor"), and the *Songshi*, ch. 1 ("Annals of Emperor Taizu").

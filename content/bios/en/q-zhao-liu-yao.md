# Liu Yao, Sovereign of Zhao (Former Zhao) · Principal Deeds

Liu Yao, styled Yongming, was a cousin of Liu Yuan's line. After the Jin Zhun troubles he took the imperial throne at Chang'an, renamed the realm Zhao (Former Zhao to history), and made Chang'an his capital. Sharp from youth, fond of books and skilled in prose, he founded an academy, cast an armillary sphere, and appointed historians — Guanzhong's culture revived for a time. But he too loved killing: the records say he "made sport of destroying whole families," and the Di and Qiang of Guanzhong rose and were put down again and again.

In the 3rd year of Taining he met Shi Le of Later Zhao in battle west of Luoyang. Drunk, he rode out at disadvantage, his horse stumbled into a stone ditch, and he was taken and carried to Xiangguo. Shi Le made him write to his son Xi, ordering the realm to surrender; Yao refused, and was put to death. The next year his sons Xi and Yin fell back to Shanggui and were destroyed by Shi Le — Former Zhao was no more.

Yao's courage exceeded his counsel, which fell short of Shi Le's: he fixed his capital at Chang'an without the support of the east, the Hu and the Han were estranged, and the people's hearts were not with him — one defeat at Luoyang ended the state and the man. The historians say he was "sharp from youth and of more than common courage when grown," but "government lost the way and the people scattered": Former Zhao fell for want of men's hearts.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 103 (records of Liu Yao), ch. 104 (records of Shi Le).

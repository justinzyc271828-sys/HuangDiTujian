# Ogedei Khan, Emperor Taizong of Yuan · Major Deeds

Borjigin Ogedei (1186-1241), third son of Genghis Khan, was raised to the throne by the kurultai in 1229 after his brother Tolui's two-year regency, and fixed the capital at Karakorum.

His reign saw the empire's greatest expansion: in 1234 his generals, with Song help, destroyed Jin at Caizhou; in 1237 he sent Batu west to break the Qipchaq and the Rus principalities, with raiding columns reaching Poland and Hungary. He himself never reached Caizhou, yet the ruin of Jin and the western marches both belong to his reign.

In governance he employed Yelü Chucai, setting taxes, founding the Secretariat, and applying Chinese institutions - the Mongol state turning from steppe empire toward a dynasty ruling settled lands. He also built up Karakorum and sent envoys westward.

He died at Karakorum in 1241; Empress Toregene ruled during the interval. Succession strife after his death began to fray the empire's cohesion.

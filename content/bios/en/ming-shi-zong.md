# Zhu Houcong, Emperor Shizong of Ming · Major Deeds

Zhu Houcong (1507-1566), son of the Prince of Xing and cousin of [[ming-wu-zong|Emperor Wuzong]], entered the line of succession in 1521, era Jiajing.

He began with vigor, punishing the eunuchs and proposing reform; but the Great Ritual Controversy - honoring his birth father as imperial father - raged for years with beatings and demotions, alienating the court. From mid-reign he turned to Daoist rites, dwelling in the Western Park for twenty years without court; Yan Song ruled for two decades, the border rotted, palace maids tried to strangle him in 1542, and in 1550 Altan Khan's cavalry reached the walls of the capital.

He died in the Qianqing Palace in 1566. The Ming decline truly began with Jiajing.

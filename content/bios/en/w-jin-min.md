# Emperor Min of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Ye, styled Yanqi, was the son of Sima Yan, Prince Xiao of Wu, adopted into the line of Sima Jian, Prince of Qin. When word of Emperor Huai's murder reached Chang'an he took the throne there at fourteen, changing the era to Jianxing. But Guanzhong lay waste, famine and plague alternating: Liu Yao besieged the city year after year, the grain ran out, a bushel of rice cost two jin of gold, and men ate men; the people died by the half.

In the eleventh month of the 4th year of Jianxing Liu Yao took the city, and the emperor, bare-armed with the seal in his mouth and the coffin in his cart, surrendered — Western Jin, through four sovereigns and fifty-two years, was no more. Brought to Pingyang, he was made to serve wine and wash the cups for Liu Cong, and in the 1st year of Jianwu was put to death at eighteen. That same year Sima Rui styled himself King of Jin at Jiankang and took the throne the next year: Eastern Jin began.

Three years on the throne, Min committed no fault; he simply had nothing to work with — a few dozen li of territory, a few thousand men. The historians write that Western Jin fell not in Min's reign but in Wu's enfeoffment of the princes and Hui's accession; Min only inherited the disaster. A child sovereign in a realm in ruins, his fate matched Huai's, and the two are joined in history as "the Huai–Min tragedy," the opening wound of the southern Jin.

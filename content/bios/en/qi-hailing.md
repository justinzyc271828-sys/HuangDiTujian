# The Prince of Hailing · Principal Deeds

Xiao Zhaowen, second son of the Crown Prince Wenhui and younger brother of the Prince of Yulin, was set up as emperor by Xiao Luan after the Yulin deposition in the 1st year of Longchang, changing the era to Yanxing — and reigned a few months. In the seventh month of the 1st year of Yanxing, Luan deposed him in turn as Prince of Hailing and took the throne himself, changing the era to Jianwu; the prince was soon done to death, aged fifteen.

His enthronement was pure transition for Luan's usurpation: one emperor deposed, another set up, another deposed — all within months. Zhaowen, "a child figurehead," sat while "Luan decided everything, the emperor folding his hands"; on his deposition he was "granted a band of music, after the precedent of Emperor Gao," and left the palace in the dusk. Though he stands in the Qi imperial sequence, his reign was the shortest and the most puppet-like of the southern sovereigns.

The historians say: "A child on the throne, and the realm's power was already gone." From Gao's founding to Hailing's deposition was but sixteen years; Luan, usurping as a senior prince, deposed two emperors and slew both (Yulin and Hailing) — cruelty beyond Xiao Daocheng's. Hailing's brief reign reflects the southern norm: the throne itself had become a thing in the strong minister's hand.

# Emperor Shang of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Long was the youngest son of [[e-han-he|Emperor He of Han]]. In the twelfth month of the first Yuanxing year, Emperor He died, and Empress Deng Sui brought in the infant Liu Long as emperor — barely a hundred days old at his accession, the youngest emperor ever enthroned in Chinese imperial history.

He reigned less than a year, and government was from the start in the hands of Deng Sui's regency: the empress dowager ruled as a woman in her own right, and the histories credit the Yanping years with a measure of lenient order — all her doing, nothing to do with the hundred-day emperor. In the eighth month of Yanping 1 he died, having reigned under a year, and was buried at Kangling.

His enthronement and his death are the first extreme specimen of Eastern Han's child-emperor politics: the sovereign had become too young to be any kind of political subject, and imperial power was merely a title in the hands of an empress dowager and her kin. Deng then chose Hu, son of the prince of Qinghe, as [[e-han-an|Emperor An]] — from then on, every succession in Eastern Han passed through a decision made behind the palace gates.

# Emperor He of Qi · Principal Deeds

Xiao Baorong, eighth son of Emperor Ming, enfeoffed Prince of Nankang. In the 2nd year of Yongyuan Xiao Yan rose from Xiangyang, and the next year set Baorong on the throne at Jiangling, changing the era to Zhongxing and styling the Marquis of Donghun in Jiankang "the deposed emperor" — Qi had two sovereigns at once. In the 1st year of Zhongxing Yan's army took Jiankang and slew the Marquis; in the third month of the 2nd year of Zhongxing, Yan's men pressed for the abdication.

The edict made, Emperor He yielded, enfeoffed Duke of Baling; within days Yan's men did him to death, aged fifteen — Qi, seven sovereigns and twenty-four years, was no more, and Xiao Yan founded Liang. A child enthroned and slain by Xiao Yan, his fate mirrors Emperor Shun of Song: the name of abdication masking the usurpation, the abdicated sovereign slain to complete it — the southern "abdication" had become a fixed ritual.

The histories absolve him: "a child in a perilous seat," his abdication and his death were not his doing. The four southern dynasties — Song, Qi, Liang, Chen — all took the realm by "abdication," and most of the abdicated sovereigns were murdered: Emperor Gong of Jin, Emperor Shun of Song, Emperor He of Qi — none died in his bed. The falseness of "abdication" was exposed to the full in the southern courts. Qi was brief, but Liang followed, and the age passed to Emperor Wu of Liang.

# Emperor Huizong of Yuan (Toghon Temur) · Major Deeds

Borjigin Toghon Temur (1320-1370), eldest son of Mingzong, drifted through Guangxi and the marches after his father's sudden death. When Ningzong died in 1333, El Temur brought him to the throne, era Yuantong - Emperor Huizong (called Shundi by the Ming).

His thirty-six years were the longest of any Yuan emperor. Under Bayan came proscription and the suspension of examinations; under Toqto'a the Zhizheng renewal - examinations restored, canals dug, the Three Histories compiled - a brief revival. But in 1351 the Red Turbans rose at Yingzhou and the realm erupted; in 1368 the Ming took Dadu and Huizong fled north to Shangdu, ending Yuan rule in China and beginning the Northern Yuan.

He died at Yingchang in 1370, given the temple name Huizong in the north. A dynasty that began with the Zhizheng renewal and ended in flight to Yingchang.

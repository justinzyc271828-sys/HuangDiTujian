# Emperor Ming of Northern Zhou · Principal Deeds

Yuwen Yu, eldest son of Yuwen Tai and elder brother of Xiaomin, was set up by Yuwen Hu after Xiaomin's fall, taking the heavenly kingship. In the 1st year of Wucheng he took the title of emperor and opened the era — the formal empire of Northern Zhou begins with Ming. He was "generous, kind, learned, and a poet," "skilled in prose," often "debating verse with his ministers" — the most cultivated of the Zhou sovereigns.

But in his three years the armies and the realm were decided wholly by Hu, who "without learning, did as he pleased"; Ming, for all his wish, had not an inch of power. In the 2nd year of Wucheng, Hu had poison put in his food; Ming died of it at twenty-seven, his last will naming his brother Yong — "the emperor, knowing Hu meant to kill him, willed the throne to Yong" — and the revival of Zhou began there.

The histories say: "generous and kind, and poisoned by the strong minister." Of the first three Zhou sovereigns, two died by Yuwen Hu's hand; Ming, the poet on the captive throne, is the more to be pitied. His will chose the hero of the age — his death was, in the end, Zhou's good fortune.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Zhoushu*, ch. 4 ("Benji of Emperor Ming").

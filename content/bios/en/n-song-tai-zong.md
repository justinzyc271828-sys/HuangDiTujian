# Zhao Guangyi, Emperor Taizong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Guangyi was the younger brother of [[n-song-tai-zu|Emperor Taizu of Song]]. When Taizu died suddenly in 976 he succeeded, changing the era to Taiping Xingguo. Brother-to-brother succession is one of the most sensitive passages in Song history - the night rumors of the "candle shadow and the axe sound" and the suspicious "Golden Coffer Pact" remain disputed; this page mentions them without asserting them.

Taizong's chief military achievement was completing unification. In 979 he campaigned in person, took Taiyuan, and destroyed [[b-han-liu-jun|Northern Han]], ending the era of the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms. That same year he pressed north against Liao and suffered a disastrous defeat at the Gaoliang River near modern Beijing, fleeing wounded by cart - a turning point in Song-Liao relations. In 986 a three-pronged campaign failed when Cao Bin was routed at Qigou Pass; northern expansion never recovered.

In domestic politics he exalted letters, expanded the examinations, and compiled the great encyclopedias, cementing civil-bureaucratic rule; but his suspicion was deep, and brothers, sons, and old ministers were pushed aside in turn.

He died in 997 and was succeeded by his third son Zhao Heng, [[n-song-zhen|Emperor Zhenzong]].

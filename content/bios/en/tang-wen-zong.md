# Emperor Wenzong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Ang, first named Han, was the second son of Muzong. In the 2nd year of Baoli, after Jingzong's slaying, the eunuch Wang Shoucheng and the others brought in Ang, changing the era to Dahe. Wenzong was "frugal and scholarly," and, reading history, sighed that "Emperor Xian of Han was bound by strong ministers, and I am bound by my own servants" — his aim was the eunuchs; but "all about him were eunuchs," and every plot leaked.

In the 5th year of Dahe the chancellor Song Shenxi plotted against the eunuchs, the plot leaked, and he died in exile at Kaizhou — the first failure. In the 9th year of Dahe, Wenzong with Li Xun and Zheng Zhu plotted: they feigned sweet dew falling on the left offices of the Guard, to lure the eunuch Qiu Shiliang and the rest within for slaughter — the Ganlu incident. But the secret broke; Qiu Shiliang and the others struck first, slaying Li Xun, Zheng Zhu, and over a thousand courtiers — "the blood ran in rivers"; and from that day Wenzong was bound by the eunuchs, "an emperor who could not keep even his own attendants."

He died in the 5th year of Kaicheng, aged thirty-two. The histories say: "frugal and fond of letters, his will to destroy the eunuchs defeated." The Ganlu incident was the summit of eunuch power: from it, "the enthroning and deposing of emperors came all from the inner court." A gentle sovereign seeking to sweep away the old plague — his will is to be honored, his failure mourned: after Ganlu, the government of Tang passed to the northern court of the eunuchs.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 17b ("Benji of Emperor Wenzong").

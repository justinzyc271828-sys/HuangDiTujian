# Emperor Wenxuan of Northern Qi · Principal Deeds

Gao Yang, styled Zijin, was the second son of Gao Huan. In the 1st year of Tianbao, Emperor Xiaojing of Eastern Wei abdicated; Yang took the throne, styled the realm Qi, and made Ye his capital — the beginning of Northern Qi. At first he governed with vigor: "on taking the throne he gave real care to the arts of government," clearing away the evils of Wei's last years and employing the Han gentry, Yang Yin and the rest; he campaigned north in person against the mountain Hu, the Khitan, and the Kumo Xi, carrying the border beyond the Great Wall; in the 6th year of Tianbao he drove the people to build the Wall from the Xi River to the sea — the martial glory of Northern Qi began with Wenxuan.

But in his last years he was known for drunken madness: "in his final years he sank into lewdness and cruelty," killing in his cups, playing the soldier in the streets, ungovernable in his folly; he "raised the three terraces, of the utmost splendor." The histories note the same man, clear in his first years and mad in his last — one of the great oddities of Northern Qi politics.

He died in the 10th year of Tianbao at thirty-one; his son Yin succeeded. The histories say of him: "hero and tyrant in one." The foundation of Qi was laid in his early government; the tradition of Qi's tyranny also began with his last years — his drunken slaughter opened the line of tyrants, and the darkness of Wucheng and the Last Sovereign followed.

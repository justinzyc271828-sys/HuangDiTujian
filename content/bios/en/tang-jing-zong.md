# Emperor Jingzong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Zhan, eldest son of Muzong, took the throne on his father's death in the 4th year of Changqing at sixteen, changing the era to Baoli. Jingzong was "a child at heart": he loved polo, wrestling, and cockfighting, sat late at court, hunted by night, "playing without limit"; the government fell to the eunuchs, Wang Shoucheng and the rest, and "the government lay with the eunuchs" was ever more fixed.

In the 2nd year of Baoli, returning from a night hunt, he drank with the eunuchs Liu Keming and Su Zuoming, and, drunk, was slain by Su Zuoming and the others, aged eighteen — the first Tang emperor slain by eunuchs. The eunuchs under Wang Shoucheng in turn slew the murderers and brought in the Prince of Jiang, Li Han (Wenzong).

The histories say: "a child at heart, he died by the eunuchs' hand." The eunuch disaster of Tang reached its height with Jingzong: enthroning, slaying, deposing emperors — all the eunuchs' work. A playing sovereign in the eunuch age, his death was his own making; but the binding of Wenzong after the Ganlu incident was the same pattern continued. The decline of the Tang throne was plain from Jingzong's slaying.

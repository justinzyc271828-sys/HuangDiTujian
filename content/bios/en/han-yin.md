# The Yin Emperor of Later Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Chengyou, second son of Gaozu, took the throne on his father's death in the 1st year of Qianyou at eighteen. The Yin emperor was "a child," and on accession left the government to the regents Yang Bin, Shi Hongzhao, and Wang Zhang; in the 2nd year of Qianyou Li Shouzhen rose at Hezhong, with Zhao Siwan and Wang Jingchong following — three garrisons in league — put down by Guo Wei, whose prestige from that day rose daily.

In the 3rd year of Qianyou, heeding the slanders of Su Fengji and the rest, the Yin emperor slew Yang Bin, Shi Hongzhao, and Wang Zhang in the palace, and sent secret orders against Guo Wei, garrison commander of Yedu; Guo Wei, learning of it, marched south. The emperor's armies went over to him; Guo Wei's host entered Kaifeng, and the Yin emperor, fleeing, was slain by the mutineers, aged twenty — Later Han was no more, two emperors and four years.

The histories say: "a child heeding slander, he destroyed his own shield." Later Han fell to the Yin emperor's credulity and slaughter: the regents destroyed in a day, Guo Wei driven to revolt — the age's worst "pulling down one's own wall." Guo Wei, entering Kaifeng, first set up the Han prince Liu Yun (later deposed) and then took the throne himself as the realm Zhou — the Five Dynasties passed to Later Zhou.

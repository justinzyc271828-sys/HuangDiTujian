# Zhao Yun, Emperor Lizong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Yun, tenth-generation descendant of [[n-song-tai-zu|Emperor Taizu]], was raised by Shi Miyuan's forged edict in 1224, era Baoqing.

In 1234 Song-Mongol forces destroyed Jin at Caizhou; [[jin-ai-zong|Emperor Aizong of Jin]] hanged himself and Jin fell. After Shi Miyuan's death Lizong governed in person: he changed the era to Duanping, posthumously honored Zhu Xi and the Cheng brothers, and carried Neo-Confucianism to its official apogee - the "reverence for dao-learning" that defined his political style. That same year, however, his troops marched to recover the Three Capitals, the "Duanping recovery," and were routed by the Mongols; the Song-Mongol compact broke and Mongolia became the great neighbor. Lizong's energy then waned; in his later years he entrusted [[jia-si-dao|Jia Sidao]] while Mongol armies pressed south.

He died in 1264; his nephew Zhao Qi became [[s-song-du|Emperor Duzong]].

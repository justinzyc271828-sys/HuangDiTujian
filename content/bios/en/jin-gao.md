# Emperor Gaozu of Later Jin · Principal Deeds

Shi Jingtang, a Shatuo, was the son-in-law of Mingzong and governor of Hedong. In the 3rd year of Qingtai (936), when the Last Sovereign moved and stripped his garrisons, Jingtang revolted: he begged aid of the Khitan, acknowledged the Khitan ruler Yelü Deguang as "father emperor," called himself "son emperor," and promised the Sixteen Prefectures of Yanyun — the name "son emperor" passing into the eternal mockery of history. The Khitan helped him destroy Later Tang; Jingtang took the throne at Taiyuan as the realm Jin, with its capital at Kaifeng.

In the 3rd year of Tianfu (938) the Khitan took the name Liao, and Jingtang sent the maps and registers of the Sixteen Prefectures — the lands from Youzhou (Beijing) to Yunzhou (Datong), sixteen prefectures in all, passed to the Khitan; from that day "the northern border was vassal and tribute," the Jin paying tens of thousands in gold and silk each year. The Jin realm was in truth Liao's dependency.

In the 7th year of Tianfu Jingtang died, aged fifty-one. The histories say: "he served the Khitan with his lands — the histories call him the son emperor." Jingtang's crime was not the throne (any warlord of the age might be emperor) but the cession of Yanyun and the vassal's name — with the Sixteen Prefectures, the Central Plains lost the shield of the Great Wall, and "the balance of the realm ran from north to south" from that day; the disaster ran on into Song and Ming, the greatest stain of the age.

# Emperor Ruizong of Tang (Restored) · Principal Deeds

Li Dan, restored after the Tanglong coup. In the 1st year of Jingyun, Li Longji slew Empress Wei and brought Ruizong back to the throne, changing the era to Jingyun — his second reign. At the restoration he made Longji, whose merit had put down Wei, crown prince; but the government fell again under the Princess Taiping (Wu's daughter, Ruizong's sister): "whatever the princess wished, the emperor granted," and he "could not decide for the princess's sake."

In the 1st year of Xiantian he yielded the throne to the heir Longji (Xuanzong) and styled himself Grand Emperor — "yielding to his worthy son," the one bright deed of his life. But the Princess Taiping's power was great; she had urged him not to abdicate, and now plotted against Xuanzong; in the 2nd year of Xiantian the emperor struck first, had the princess put to death, and the Grand Emperor gave up the government entirely. He died in the Baifu Hall in the 4th year of Kaiyuan, aged fifty-five.

The histories say of his restoration: "he yielded to his worthy son — a good name in history." Twice on the throne, twice a puppet (of Wu, of the princess); but he knew his son, and his yielding opened the Kaiyuan golden age. The turn of Tang was his abdication — "knowing when to withdraw" is the whole of his life.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 7 ("Benji of Emperor Ruizong"), ch. 8 ("Benji of Emperor Xuanzong").

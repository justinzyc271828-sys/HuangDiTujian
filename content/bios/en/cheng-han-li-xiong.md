# Li Xiong, Grand Ancestor of Cheng Han · Principal Deeds

Li Xiong, styled Zhongjun, was the son of Li Te, leader of the Ba-Di refugee host. In the 2nd year of Tai'an, Li Te, fighting the Yi Province governor Luo Shang, was defeated and slain; Xiong took command and held off Luo Shang. In the 1st year of Jianxing he styled himself King of Chengdu and opened an era; in the 1st year of Yanping he took the imperial throne, styling the realm Dacheng with its capital at Chengdu. Cheng Han stood beside the states of the Central Plains — the one of the Sixteen that held Bashu alone.

In thirty years Xiong's rule was the most benign of the age: light levies, easy justice, "tax and corvée both spared, the people prosperous," and the people of Shu at peace. He had no son and raised his nephew Ban as heir; when he died in the 24th year of Yuheng and Ban succeeded, Xiong's own sons would not bear it — Li Qi and the others murdered Ban, and Cheng Han began its decline. His benevolence could not bequeath itself.

The historians say: "With a measure of benevolence Xiong nourished the refugees, and so held Bashu for nearly thirty years." Amid the tyrants of the Sixteen States his rule stands out; but the succession was unsecured and his sons tore the state — the good rule was a brief spring. Huan Wen of Eastern Jin later destroyed Cheng Han, and the enclave ended.

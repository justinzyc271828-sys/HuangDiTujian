# The Marquis of Donghun · Principal Deeds

Xiao Baojuan, second son of Emperor Ming, took the throne in the 1st year of Yongtai at sixteen, changing the era to Yongyuan. The Marquis of Donghun was the worst tyrant of the southern dynasties: the records tell of his "love of play," his cruelty; on the throne he slew his ministers — Xu Xiaosi, Chen Xianda, and the rest — and "had gold beaten into lotus petals to pave the ground, and made Consort Pan walk on them, saying, 'with every step a lotus grows.'" The golden lotus and the favorite Pan passed into legend.

In the Yongyuan years he "kept himself hidden," leaving the government to the favorites Ru Fazhen and Mei Chong'er; he slew princes and meritorious ministers without measure, until Xiao Yan, the Governor of Yongzhou, rose from Xiangyang and marched east to punish him. In the 1st year of Zhongxing Yan's army entered Jiankang; Baojuan was degraded as Marquis of Donghun and slain by his own officers, aged nineteen — the enterprise of Gao and Wu was cut off.

The histories say: "of slow speech, keeping to himself," yet "cruel, extravagant, and lawless beyond measure." From Gao's founding, Qi ran seven sovereigns and twenty-four years — Luan's usurpation and the princes' slaughter, the Marquis's tyranny and the realm's desertion: Xiao Yan's succession was only the last step. The Marquis's fall is another instance of the southern tyrant's swift ruin; the golden lotus and the slaughter remain the eternal warning.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Nanqishu*, ch. 7 (the Marquis of Donghun), and the *Liangshu*, ch. 1 ("Benji of Emperor Wu").

# Emperor Wen of Wei · Principal Deeds

Cao Pi, styled Zihuan, was Cao Cao's second son by Lady Bian. Gifted early in letters, fond of literature, and skilled at riding and archery, he stood with his father and Cao Zhi at the heart of the Jian'an literary circle. In the 22nd year of Jian'an (217) Cao Cao made him heir of Wei, ending the decade-long rivalry with Cao Zhi. In the first month of Yankang 1 (220) Cao Cao died and Pi inherited the kingship; that October Emperor Xian abdicated, Pi took the throne at Fanyang, changed the era to Huangchu, styled the realm Wei, and made Luoyang his capital — the four-hundred-year Han line was cut, and the Wei–Jin cycle of abdications began.

His seven years were richer in institutions than in arms: he fixed the calendar, vestments, and sacrifices, rebuilding the rites of a founding dynasty; on Chen Qun's counsel he established the nine-rank method of appointing officials, opening the era of the great clans; and he reduced the imperial princes to county marquises to guard against feudatory danger. In arms, the three-pronged campaign against Wu gained nothing, and twice he toured Guangling without ever crossing the Yangzi. His temper was harsh: Bao Xun and other remonstrants were put to death, drawing the historians' censure.

In the fifth month of Huangchu 7 he died in the Jiafu Hall at Luoyang, aged forty, and Emperor Ming (Cao Rui) succeeded. Chen Shou judged him "gifted of literary talent, writing at the brush's touch," while regretting the want of magnanimity. His *Dianlun: On Literature* founded Chinese literary criticism; "literary composition is the great enterprise of statecraft, the glorious work of the imperishable" still echoes — in both letters and institutions, Wen of Wei stands as the hinge of his age.

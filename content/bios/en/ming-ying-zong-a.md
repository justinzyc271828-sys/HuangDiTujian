# Zhu Qizhen, Emperor Yingzong of Ming (Zhengtong) · Major Deeds

Zhu Qizhen (1427-1464), eldest son of [[ming-xuan-zong|Emperor Xuanzong]], took the throne at nine in 1435, era Zhengtong.

His early years ran under the grand dowager and the Three Yangs; from mid-reign the eunuch Wang Zhen dominated and government decayed. In 1449 Wang Zhen dragged the emperor into a personal campaign against the Oirats; in August, at Tumu, the army was shattered and Yingzong captured - the Tumu Incident. The Oirats pressed the capital with the emperor in train.

His younger brother [[ming-dai-zong|the Prince of Cheng]] took the throne (Jingtai), and Yu Qian held Beijing against Esen. Released the next year, Yingzong returned as retired emperor, confined in the Southern Palace. The humiliation of his captivity was unprecedented in Ming history.

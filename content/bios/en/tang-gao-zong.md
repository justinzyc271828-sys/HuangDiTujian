# Emperor Gaozong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Zhi, styled Weishan, was the ninth son of Taizong by Empress Zhangsun. On Taizong's death in the 23rd year of Zhenguan he took the throne, changing the era to Yonghui. The Yonghui years carried on the Zhenguan legacy: "the government of Yonghui favored leniency and simplicity," with Zhangsun Wuji, Chu Suiliang, and the others as regents and the law in good order — the *Yonghui Code* was issued, the masterpiece of Tang law.

In the 6th year of Yonghui he deposed Empress Wang and made the Consort Wu his empress — the "deposition for Wu" was the great turn of the Tang court: from that day Wu entered the government, and the emperor's power ebbed. In the 5th year of Xianqing, plagued by vertigo, he had the offices report to Wu for decision, "all the power of the realm passing to the inner palace." Yet the arms of his reign were strong: in the 2nd year of Xianqing the Western Turks were destroyed, and in the 1st year of Zongzhang Li Ji subdued Goguryeo, setting up the Protectorate of Andong — the widest borders of Tang came in Gaozong's reign.

He died in the Zhenguan Hall at the eastern capital in the 1st year of Hongdao, aged fifty-six. The histories say: "Yonghui carried the Zhenguan air; after his middle years the government passed to Empress Wu." Gentle and weak, he inherited the Zhenguan foundations and opened the way for Wu: his reign was the hinge from Tang to the Zhou — the rise of the Wu Zhou was Wu's ambition, but also the fruit of his delegation.

# Emperor Xianwen of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Tuoba Hong, eldest son of Emperor Wencheng, took the throne on his father's death in the 6th year of Heping at eleven, with Empress Dowager Feng holding court. In mid-Huangxing he slew the usurping minister Yi Hun and ruled in person. Xianwen "loved the learning of Huang–Lao and the Buddha, thought lightly of wealth and rank, and had always the mind of one withdrawing from the world" — the histories say he had no heart for the throne.

In the 5th year of Huangxing he abdicated to his heir Hong (Emperor Xiaowen) and styled himself Grand Emperor — an abdication at seventeen, rare in all history. Yet after the abdication "the great affairs of the realm were still reported to him," and the Grand Emperor kept a hand in government; Empress Feng returned to power, and she and Xianwen disagreed on much. In the 1st year of Chengming he died suddenly at Pingcheng, aged twenty-three; the histories point at Empress Feng.

His abdication, whether high-minded or compelled, brought Xiaowen early to the throne and advanced the sinicization reforms by a generation. The histories say of him: "sharp from youth, benevolent and generous when grown," yet "soaked in the Buddha, he came to abdicate." The struggle between the throne and the empress dowager's power grew fierce in his reign — the thread that runs through Xiaowen's reforms and Xuanwu's Buddhism.

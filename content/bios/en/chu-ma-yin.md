# Ma Yin, King Wumu of Chu · Principal Deeds

Ma Yin, a man of Yanling in Xuzhou, entered Hunan in mid-Qianning with Sun Ru's remnant and held Tanzhou; in the 1st year of Kaiping (907) Later Liang made him King of Chu, holding Hunan — the beginning of Chu. Ma Yin was "generous by nature"; with Xu Dexun and Gao Yu as his counselors he promoted the tea trade — "the people free to gather and sell tea, the northern merchants taking it, a million a year" — and minted iron coins that worked within the realm only, so that the merchants' profit fell to Chu: the wealth of Chu rose from tea.

Over twenty years he kept his borders and opened his commerce: serving Liang and Tang in the north, soothing the cave peoples in the south, "the people of Hunan gaining rest." But in his last years the sons' contention was already seeded; he died in the 1st year of Changxing (930), aged seventy-nine, and his sons fell to fighting — Chu declined.

The histories say: "he kept his borders and opened the trade, and Hunan was at peace." Chu, a realm built on tea, was the most commercial of the Ten; but after Ma Yin's death "the sons contended, brothers slaying brothers," and within two generations Chu fell to Southern Tang in 951. Chu rose on commerce and fell on discord — one lesson of the age.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 66 ("Annals of Chu").

# Emperor Wu of Han · Principal Deeds

The length of Liu Che's reign, the abundance of his era names, and the breadth of his expansion make him a pivot that no reader of Western Han history can bypass. This page takes the "Annals of Emperor Wu" in the Book of Han as its chronological spine, supplemented by relevant chapters of the Records of the Grand Historian and the chronicle of the Zizhi Tongjian.

He strengthened central authority, exalted Confucian learning, and developed the northern frontier and the Western Regions, leaving a complex legacy of border defenses, fiscal strain, and a late-reign change of course. Read alongside [[qin-shi-huang|Qin Shi Huang]]: one man completed the first great unification and fixed the institution of the emperor; the other carried the unified empire's military reach, ritual order, and fiscal apparatus to new heights. Read alongside [[han-gao-zu|Emperor Gaozu of Han]]: one sees the span of the Han house from its rough founding to its full zenith.

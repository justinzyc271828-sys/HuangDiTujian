# Emperor Wencheng of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Tuoba Jun was the grandson of Emperor Taiwu. In the 2nd year of Zhengping the eunuch Zong Ai slew Emperor Taiwu and then the heir Huang; Zong Ai was in turn slain, and Jun entered, steadied the realm, and took the throne at thirteen, changing the era to Xing'an. He reversed Taiwu's harshness: in the 2nd year of Xing'an he restored the Buddhist law by edict, "allowing the people to take orders," and the faith, extinguished by Taiwu, revived; in the 1st year of Heping, at Tanyao's plea, the Wuzhou Mountain caves were opened — the Yungang grottoes, treasure of Chinese Buddhist art, were begun.

Thirteen years he reigned, his punishments lenient and his government simple, letting the people rest: "in the emperor's reign the wars slackened and the people's strength revived," and Northern Wei turned from arms toward rule. But he too raised great works and built palaces; and he made the Lady Feng his empress — the Empress Dowager Feng who would shape the Xiaowen reforms. He died in the 6th year of Heping at twenty-six; his son Hong succeeded as Emperor Xianwen.

The histories say: "divine from birth, benevolent and generous when grown." A thirteen-year-old who steadied the realm after Taiwu's sudden death, restored the faith, and opened the Yungang age — his stewardship was real. The Buddhist revival and the grottoes begin with him; and the entry of Empress Feng into the palace laid the thread of the sinicization to come.

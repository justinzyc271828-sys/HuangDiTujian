# Emperor Xizong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Xuan, first named Yan, was the fifth son of Yizong. On Yizong's death in the 14th year of Xiantong the eunuch Tian Lingzi and the others set up Xuan, aged twelve, changing the era to Qianfu. Xizong "played from childhood," leaving everything to Tian Lingzi, whom he called "foster father" — a thing the histories rarely record. In the 2nd year of Qianfu, Wang Xianzhi and Huang Chao rose in succession in Cao and Pu, and the realm fell into chaos.

In the 1st year of Guangming, Huang Chao crossed the Huai, took Luoyang, and entered Chang'an; Xizong fled to Shu (Chengdu) — the second exile of the Tang house. Huang Chao took the capital as emperor of the Great Qi; in the 3rd year of Zhonghe Li Keyong and the Shatuo troops broke Chao and recovered Chang'an, and Xizong returned. But the Tang after the storm: "the feudatories fought each other, the eunuchs fought among themselves," and when Tian Lingzi clashed with Wang Chongrong, Xizong fled again to Fengxiang.

He died after returning to the capital in the 1st year of Wende, aged twenty-seven. The histories say: "a child in exile, the Tang near its fall." The Huang Chao rebellion was the direct prelude to the fall: the feudatories grew stronger than ever (Zhu Wen and Li Keyong were both rebel generals), the eunuch disaster worse — "the fall of Tang was truly prepared in Xizong's reign."

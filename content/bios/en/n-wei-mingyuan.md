# Emperor Mingyuan of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Tuoba Si was the eldest son of Emperor Daowu. In the 6th year of Tianci the Prince of Qinghe slew Daowu; Si, hearing the news abroad, entered Pingcheng, put down the rebellion, slew Shao, and took the throne, changing the era to Yongxing. He carried on Daowu's work, holding the realm and extending it: in the 1st year of Taichang, as Liu Yu campaigned against Later Qin, the Wei armies took Huatai, Hulao, and the rest; in the 7th year he held Si, Yan, Yu, and the lands north of the Huai — the southern frontier ran along the Huai from that day, and the standoff of north and south was fixed.

His government aimed at stability: he made his heir Tao regent — the "heir's regency" that began Northern Wei's succession system — and lightened the levies and comforted the refugees. But his health failed; he died in the 8th year of Taichang at thirty-two, and his heir Tao succeeded as Emperor Taiwu.

The histories say of him: "sharp, gentle, of both letters and arms." A steward who, in the moment of Liu Yu's great expedition, extended his borders and reaped where others sowed — his vision matched Daowu's own. His reign made the state rich and strong; the unification of the north under Taiwu stood on the foundations his father and he had laid.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 3 ("Benji of Emperor Mingyuan").

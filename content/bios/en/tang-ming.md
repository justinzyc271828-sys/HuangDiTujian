# Emperor Mingzong of Later Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Siyuan, a Shatuo of Daibei, originally Miaojiilie, was the adopted son of Li Keyong. A fierce warrior, he was a founding general of Later Tang. In the 4th year of Tongguang, driven to revolt by Zhuangzong's suspicion, he was carried by the Weizhou mutineers; Zhuangzong fell to an arrow, and Siyuan entered Luoyang and took the throne, changing the era to Tiancheng. His government was "lenient and humane": the histories say he "attended to the people's affairs and lightened the levies," "the most humane of the Five Dynasties' sovereigns," and call his reign "the one modest peace of the Five Dynasties."

Seven years he reigned, at peace with the Khitan, Wu, and Shu on his borders, resting the people of the Central Plains amid the storms. But "the feudatories were hard to control and the succession unclear": in the 4th year of Changxing, as he lay ill, his son Congrong contended for the succession and was slain, and Conghou (the Min emperor) took the throne as Mingzong died, aged sixty-seven.

The histories say: "lenient government, stilling the wars — the Five Dynasties' one peace." An adopted son who won the throne and kept it with benevolence, the rarest worthy of the age; but "the old abuses ran too deep for one man to mend" — after his death the realm fell at once into the Min and Mo emperors' wars, and Later Tang was gone within three years. The chaos of the age could not be stopped by one sovereign.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 35–44 ("Annals of Emperor Mingzong").

# Emperor Hui of Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Ying was the son of [[han-gao-zu|Emperor Gaozu of Han]] and Empress Lü. He succeeded to the throne as heir apparent while still young, keeping the statutes of Xiao He and the policies of Cao Shen and resting the people. Yet from the first day his reign stood in the shadow of his mother, Empress Dowager Lü: the building of Chang'an, the visits of the feudatory kings, and the marriage alliance with the Xiongnu were all decided with her participation. The emperor himself was known for gentleness and weakness, and the histories tell us far less about his seven years of rule than about Lü's regency.

His reign was not, however, a blank. The repeal of the law against private possession of books relaxed the cultural prohibitions inherited from Qin. The completion of the walls of Chang'an gave the new capital its first full circuit of fortifications. And the continued marriage alliance with the Xiongnu preserved the frontier quiet on which early Han rested. None of these measures made a great narrative, but together they prepared the ground for the celebrated Wen-Jing recovery.

Emperor Hui died in the Weiyang Palace at the age of twenty-four and was buried at Anling. After his death Lü enthroned a young emperor and assumed the regency in her own right; the Liu kings were nearly destroyed one by one, until the accession of [[han-wen-di|Emperor Wen of Han]] returned the House of Han to its proper line. The historians called Hui "gentle and weak," yet in what his reign accumulated, he may be read against Emperor Wen's over the long term.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Hanshu*, ch. 2 (Benji of Emperor Hui) and ch. 3 (Benji of Empress Lü), the *Shiji* "Basic Annals of Empress Lü," and Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

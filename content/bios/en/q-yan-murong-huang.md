# Murong Huang, Emperor Wenming of Former Yan · Principal Deeds

Murong Huang, styled Yuanzhen, was the son of Murong Hui, chief of the Murong Xianbei. In the 8th year of Xianhe Hui died; Huang succeeded as Duke of Liaodong and put down the rising of his brothers; in the 3rd year of Xiankang he styled himself King of Yan — the kingship of Former Yan began. He opened an era, installed officers, built Longcheng as his capital, opened up Liaoxi, broke the Yuwen tribe and struck the Duan, and his hegemony of Liaodong was fixed.

He ruled with both Hu and Han: the Han gentry Pei Ni, Feng Yi, and the rest were his counselors; he encouraged farming and mulberries, founded schools, and "in his leisure from government examined the students in person"; in arms he leaned on his brothers Murong Han and Murong Ke, generals of the first rank. The rise of the Murong house began with Huang. He died in the 4th year of Yonghe, posthumously Emperor Wenming, and his son Jun succeeded.

From one corner of Liaodong Huang built the foundations of Former Yan, "his authority and virtue together drawing the gentry and the people." His son Jun destroyed Ran Wei and entered the Central Plains; his grandson Wei reigned at the height of Former Yan — the work of Huang's founding. But he distrusted his brothers, driving Murong Han away, and the seed of the house's internal wars was sown here: Former Yan rose swiftly and fell swiftly, and the root of the ruin lay in the founder.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 109 (records of Murong Huang), ch. 110 (records of Murong Jun).

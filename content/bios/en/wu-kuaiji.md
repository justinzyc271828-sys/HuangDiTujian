# The Prince of Kuajji · Principal Deeds

Sun Liang, styled Ziming, was Sun Quan's youngest son by Empress Pan, the favorite of Quan's old age. In the 2nd year of Taiyuan, at seventy-one, Quan named Liang heir and died; Liang took the throne at nine, changing the era to Jianxing, with Zhuge Ke as regent. In the 2nd year of Jianxing Ke raised 200,000 men to besiege the new city of Hefei; plague struck and the host came back ruined, and Sun Jun, ambushing him at a banquet, slew Ke and made himself grand general, his power growing daily. In the 2nd year of Wufeng Jun put Zhang Ti and other ministers to death, acting as he pleased.

In the 1st year of Taiping Jun died of illness, and his cousin Sun Chen took the government, more overbearing still. Liang, young but sharp, had begun to attend to government himself; seeing Chen's "monopoly and arrogance," he plotted with Princess Quan and Liu Cheng to slay him. In the 3rd year of Taiping, Liang's secret order to surround Chen's mansion leaked; Chen struck first, deposed Liang as Prince of Kuajji, and brought in the Prince of Langya, Sun Xiu. In exile at Kuajji, pressed by Xiu's men in the 3rd year of Yong'an, Liang took his own life at eighteen.

The histories call Liang "sharp-witted though a child"; his own judgments of court affairs and his recognition of Sun Chen's villainy show no dullard. But a nine-year-old sovereign amid a succession of strong ministers was at the mercy of the age, and he ended deposed and dead. After Sun Quan's death Wu changed sovereigns three times in ten years — Liang, Xiu, Hao — each change by the strong ministers' will: the deposition of Sun Liang opened the era in which Wu's throne passed by coup.

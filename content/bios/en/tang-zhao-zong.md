# Emperor Zhaozong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Ye, first named Jie, was the seventh son of Yizong. On Xizong's death in the 1st year of Wende the eunuch Yang Fugong and the others set up Ye, aged twenty-two. Zhaozong "was sharp and spirited, a lover of letters," and, grieving that "the royal house was sinking," meant to restore the Tang — but his age had the feudatories strong and the eunuchs fixed, and his struggles failed again and again: in the Qianning years he warred with Li Keyong and Li Maozhen, and Chang'an burned twice.

In the 1st year of Tianfu the eunuch Han Quanhui carried Zhaozong to Fengxiang, to lean on Li Maozhen; in the 3rd year Zhu Quanzhong (Zhu Wen) attacked Fengxiang, Maozhen sued for peace and handed over the emperor and the eunuchs. Quanzhong brought the emperor back, "slaughtered the eunuchs" — the eunuch disaster was ended, but "the Tang court lived at the Bian army's mercy": Zhaozong passed from the eunuchs' hand to Zhu Quanzhong's.

In the 1st year of Tianyou Quanzhong forced the move to Luoyang, demolishing Chang'an's palaces; in the eighth month he had Jiang Xuanhui and the others slay Zhaozong in the Jiaodian Hall of Luoyang, aged thirty-eight — and Emperor Ai, the last of the Tang, was set up. The histories say: "he meant to restore the realm, and was in the end bound by Quanzhong." His realm was already like eggs piled: his will to restore is to be honored, but "the feudatories' strength and the depth of the old abuses" were beyond any one man. With his slaying, the fall of Tang was unavoidable.

# Emperor Ming of Qi · Principal Deeds

Xiao Luan, styled Jingqi, was a nephew of Emperor Gao, son of Xiao Daosheng, Prince Zhen of Shian. Serving both Song and Qi, he held the highest offices as a senior prince of tested ability. In the 1st year of Jianwu he deposed the Prince of Hailing and took the throne himself — and from that day the sons of Gao and Wu were under the knife. Four years he reigned, "slaughtering the princes": of Gao's nineteen sons and Wu's sons, nearly all were destroyed, "so that no descendant of Gao and Wu remained" — the histories call it poison.

Yet he had administrative talent: he revised the registers, verified the households, lightened the levies, and kept the government in order; in the 4th year of Jianwu, when Emperor Xiaowen of Wei marched south in force, he held the line and the border, though in peril, did not break. But his jealousy was a disease: "hiding himself deep," he set the secretaries of inspection over the princes, and in his last years, having no son, had his favorites "bear children" for him — the family cruelty carried to its last extreme.

He died in the 1st year of Yongtai at forty-seven; his son Baojuan, the Marquis of Donghun, succeeded. The histories say: "administrative ability with boundless jealousy, ruling by slaughter." A senior prince who usurped and butchered the sons of Gao and Wu, he held his throne a while — but his son's tyranny was worse, Xiao Yan rose, and within three years Qi was gone: his "poison" was the direct cause of the realm's swift fall.

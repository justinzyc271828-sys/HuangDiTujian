# Emperor Dezong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Kuo, eldest son of Daizong, took the throne in the 14th year of Dali, changing the era to Jianzhong. His first years were full of purpose: in the 1st year of Jianzhong, on Yang Yan's counsel, he introduced the two-tax system — the tax fixed by expenditure, levied in summer and autumn, a transformation of the tax order that lasted into the Ming; and he meant to cut the feudatories. In the 2nd year of Jianzhong the garrisons of Chengde, Weibo, and Ziqing joined in revolt, and the court's campaigns failed.

In the 4th year of Jianzhong came the Jingyuan mutiny: the Jingyuan troops, passing through Chang'an without their pay, mutinied and raised Zhu Ci, who took the capital as emperor. Dezong fled in haste to Fengtian — the Fengtian crisis; Zhu Ci besieged the town for over a month, held by Hun Zhen and the rest. In the 1st year of Xingyuan Dezong issued the edict of self-blame — "I have truly been wanting in virtue, and brought this calamity" — and the realm was moved; Li Sheng recovered Chang'an and Zhu Ci fell. But "the will to cut the feudatories was broken from that day."

In the Zhenyuan years he "grew daily more suspicious and ever keener on revenue": the eunuch command of the imperial armies began in his reign, and the market-depredations and the tribute-seeking were his government's ways. He died in the 21st year of Zhenyuan, aged sixty-four. The histories say: "he would cut the feudatories at first, suffered exile in the middle, and grew suspicious and grasping at the last." The two-tax system and the Fengtian crisis are his reign's great deeds — the hinge of Tang's passage from height to decline.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 12 and 13 ("Benji of Emperor Dezong").

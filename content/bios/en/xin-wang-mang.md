# Wang Mang of Xin · Principal Deeds

Wang Mang is a pivotal figure that no account of the interlude between the two Han dynasties can bypass: he climbed to the summit of power as a consort-clan kinsman, ended Western Han with his own hands, and then drove his Xin dynasty to swift ruin through a program of archaizing reform. This page takes the "Biography of Wang Mang" in the Book of Han as its chronological spine, supplemented by the "Annals of Emperor Guangwu" in the Book of the Later Han and the chronicle of the Zizhi Tongjian (available in the local reference library).

His rise entered through the Confucian gate. His aunt Wang Zhengjun was Emperor Yuan's empress; on the strength of his consort-clan standing and Confucian renown he rose step by step, being enfeoffed Duke of Anhan during the Yuanshi years and taking control of the court. After Emperor Ping's death he set up the infant Ruzi Ying and acted as regent, reenacting the precedent of the Duke of Zhou and adding the title Acting Emperor. In the first year of Chushi, portents and abdication propaganda crowded thick, smoothing the legitimacy of replacing Han. In the first year of Shijianguo (AD 9), he deposed Ruzi Ying and founded a dynasty named Xin.

Once emperor, he pressed reforms on the authority of the Rites of Zhou and other classics: he instituted "king's fields" and banned the trade in slaves, repeatedly changed the coinage and the names of offices, and deepened economic regimentation through the Five Equalizations and Six Monopolies, shaking society violently. Abroad, relations with the Xiongnu broke down, and frontier costs rose together with the ravages of war. The crack between the grandeur of the institutional blueprint and the price reality had to pay widened fast.

During the Tianfeng years, popular revolts, the Lulin, the Red Eyebrows, and others, swarmed up, and Xin's grip disintegrated; in the third year of Dihuang the Lulin forces in Jingzhou rose in strength, and local control was lost. In the fourth year of Dihuang, the Xin army was crushed at Kunyang; the Gengshi regime pressed in at once, Chang'an fell, Wang Mang died on the Jian Terrace, and Xin perished after fourteen years.

Read alongside [[e-han-guangwu|Emperor Guangwu of Han]]: one took the realm through portents and abdication and perished abruptly; the other rose at Kunyang and rebuilt the Han house. Verdicts on Wang Mang have long swung between the poles of "usurping traitor" and "idealist reformer"; this project's judgment is negative, but the value of debating his reforms is left to the future.

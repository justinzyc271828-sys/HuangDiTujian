# Liu Chong, Founder of Northern Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Chong, later renamed Liu Min, was the younger brother of Liu Zhiyuan, the Gaozu of Later Han, and governor of Hedong. In the 4th year of Qianyou (951), when Guo Wei took the Zhou throne from Han, Chong took the throne at Taiyuan as the realm Han (Northern Han to history), keeping the Qianyou era — the remnant of Later Han, holding the one corner of Hedong.

Northern Han was narrow and weak, and Chong "borrowed the Khitan to hold out against Zhou and Song": he called himself the Khitan's "nephew emperor," paying yearly tribute for support. In the 7th year of Qianyou (954), judging the new Shizong of Zhou an easy mark, he attacked Zhou in force with the Khitan; at Gaoping, with Shizong urging his army in person, the Northern Han host was shattered — "the Khitan horsemen fled without a fight." Chong escaped with his life alone, fled back to Taiyuan, and died of grief and rage, aged sixty.

The histories say: "he borrowed the Khitan to hold out against Zhou and Song." Northern Han was an island of the age: leaning on the Khitan in the north, defying Zhou and Song in the south, it lingered twenty-eight years (until Taizong of Song destroyed it in 979). Chong, "a scion of the Han house" defying Zhou, was yet the successor of the "son emperor" Shi Jingtang — "borrowing the outsider to stand, in the end bound by the outsider."

# Zhu Yijun, Emperor Shenzong of Ming · Major Deeds

Zhu Yijun (1563-1620), son of [[ming-mu-zong|Emperor Muzong]], took the throne at ten in 1572, era Wanli.

Under the guardian Zhang Juzheng came the Wanli reforms: the performance-examination system, land surveys, the Single Whip tax - the most effective reform of the late Ming, filling the treasury and disciplining officialdom. Juzheng died in 1582; Shenzong turned on his memory, confiscating his estate, then withdrew from government for thirty years. Mining-tax eunuchs ravaged the provinces and the Donglin faction wars began. In war, the Three Great Campaigns (Ningxia, Bozhou, Korea) were fought by generals; in 1619 the Sarhu defeat broke the hold on Liaodong.

He died in 1620. A first decade of governance and three of neglect - two reigns in one emperor, and the fall was seeded there.

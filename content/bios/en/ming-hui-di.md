# Zhu Yunwen, Jianwen Emperor · Major Deeds

Zhu Yunwen (1377-1402), grandson of [[ming-tai-zu|Emperor Taizu]] and son of Crown Prince Biao, took the throne in 1398, era Jianwen.

With Qi Tai, Huang Zicheng, and Fang Xiaoru he governed with leniency and pressed the reduction of the feudal princes: Zhou, Qi, Xiang, Dai, and Min were stripped in turn, the sword aimed at the Prince of Yan, [[ming-cheng-zu|Zhu Di]]. In 1399 Zhu Di rose in the "Jingnan" war from Beiping; in 1402 his armies entered Nanjing, fire rose in the palace, and the Jianwen emperor vanished - burned, some say, or fled as a tonsured wanderer. The mystery outlives the dynasty.

Four years of benevolent rule undone by haste; his disappearance and Chengzu's usurpation form the first great case of the Ming.

# Li Bian, Ancestor Lie of Southern Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Bian, originally Xu Zhigao, was a man of Xuzhou, adopted son of the Yang Wu regent Xu Wen. In the 1st year of Shengyuan (937) he received Yang Pu's abdication, taking the throne as the realm Qi, soon renamed Tang, with its capital at Jinling — Southern Tang began. In mid-Shengyuan he resumed the surname Li, took the name Bian, and claimed descent from Emperor Xianzong of Tang — the "successor of Tang" from that day.

His government aimed at peace within the borders: he made friends with Wuyue and Chu rather than war; he urged farming and mulberries, founded schools, and lightened the taxes, "the Jiang-Huai quietly at ease"; he raised the Shengyuan library of books, and the culture of the south outshone all. In the early years of the realm "the armies rested and the borders were at peace," the work of the Ancestor Lie's ordering.

He died in the 7th year of Shengyuan, aged fifty-six; his son Jing (the Yuan Zong) succeeded. The histories say: "his founding was well-ordered; his reign was brief." Southern Tang, seated at Jinling over the rich lands of the Yang Wu, became the most cultivated of the Ten Kingdoms — and the pattern was the Ancestor Lie's.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 62 ("Annals of Southern Tang").

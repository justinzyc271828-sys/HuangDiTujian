# Emperor Kang of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Yue, styled Shitong, was a son of Emperor Ming and younger brother of Emperor Cheng, enfeoffed Prince of Langya. In the 8th year of Xiankang, on Cheng's death, Yu Bing and the others — "with strong enemies abroad, a grown sovereign should be raised" — brought in Yue, changing the era to Jianyuan. Eastern Jin's practice of brother succeeding brother became the norm. Kang reigned barely two years, the government still in the Yu brothers' hands: He Chong and the others were regents, while Yu Yi, the emperor's uncle, held Wuchang and planned a northern expedition, building up Xiangyang — but he died before the enterprise could begin.

In the ninth month of the 2nd year of Jianyuan Kang died at twenty-three, and his son Sima Dan, two years old, took the throne as Emperor Mu. The one notable thing of Kang's reign was Yu Yi's northern plan: with Shi Hu of Later Zhao already failing, Yi wished to strike north, but the court could not agree, and nothing came of it. The northern enterprise then passed to Huan Wen and the Huan house.

The historians say of Kang: "Two years on the throne, the government still of the great clans." A brief reign amid strong ministers, he did nothing and did no harm. But he came to the throne by the Yu clan's "grown sovereign" plot, and left a child heir — the Eastern Jin pattern of "a child sovereign, a doubtful state" continued; Kang was but a passing figure in it, the Yu clan's power the real story of his age.

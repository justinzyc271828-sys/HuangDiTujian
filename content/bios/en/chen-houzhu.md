# The Last Sovereign of Chen · Principal Deeds

Chen Shubao, styled Yuanxiu, was the eldest son of Emperor Xuan. On his father's death in the 14th year of Taijian he took the throne, changing the era to Zhide. The Last Sovereign "drowned in wine and women, and gave no thought to government": in the Zhide years he raised the three pavilions of Linchun, Jieqi, and Wangxian, built of eaglewood and aloes, hung with gold and jade and pearls; he doted on the favorite Zhang Lihua and feasted through the nights with the "companions" Jiang Zong and Kong Fan, writing verse. His *Jade Trees and Rear-Court Flowers* — "bright halls and fragrant woods above the high towers, new gowns and painted faces, the flower of the city" — became the very song of a lost realm.

In the 3rd year of Zhenming, Emperor Wen of Sui sent He Ruobi and Han Qinhu south; the Sui armies crossed the river and the Chen host melted before them. Han Qinhu entered Jiankang by the Vermilion Bird Gate, and the Last Sovereign, with Zhang Lihua and the consort Kong, hid in the well of the Jingyang Palace, where the Sui soldiers drew him up — "the Jingyang well" passing into the mockery of the ages. Chen fell; the Southern Dynasties, Song, Qi, Liang, Chen, one hundred and sixty-nine years, were at an end, and the realm was one again.

Taken into Sui, he was made Duke of Changcheng and died at Luoyang in the 4th year of Renshou, aged fifty-two. The historians say of him: "a lost sovereign and the father of the sensuous verse"; his talent was real, but "with the gifts of a pleasure-lover on the throne of a crumbling age," ruin overtook him. Chen fell not to the strength of Sui but to his own indulgence — the *Jade Trees* remains the eternal warning.

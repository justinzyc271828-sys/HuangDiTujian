# Juqu Mengxun, King Wuxuan of Northern Liang · Principal Deeds

Juqu Mengxun was a Lushui Hu of Linsong, "learned in the histories and versed in astronomy." In the 2nd year of Longfei, with his brother Nancheng, he pushed Duan Ye to the governorship of Liangzhou against Later Liang; in the 1st year of Yong'an he slew Duan Ye and stood himself, styling himself Governor of Liangzhou and Duke of Zhangye — the real beginning of Northern Liang. He moved the capital to Guzang, took the title King of Hexi, and held most of the Hexi corridor.

His crowning deed was the destruction of Western Liang: in the 10th year of Xuanshi he broke Dunhuang, Li Xin fell in battle, and the Li house's Western Liang perished — Hexi was one. At its height Northern Liang "held Jincheng in the east and Dunhuang in the west," sent envoys to Northern Wei and to the Song court of the south, and welcomed the translator Tan Wuchen, so that Buddhism flourished. He died in the 3rd year of Yihe; his son Mujian succeeded, and in the 7th year of Chengping Emperor Taiwu of Northern Wei campaigned in person, Mujian surrendered, and Northern Liang was no more — the five Liangs ended.

The historians say of him: "bold of nature, of real courage, skilled in shifts." A Hu who slew his lord to take power, he had cunning in plenty and faith in little; yet he held Hexi more than thirty years, kept his borders and his people, and furthered letters and the faith — the longest-lived of the Five Liang states. Its fall also sounded the end of the Sixteen States age.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 129 (records of Juqu Mengxun), and the *Weishu*, biography of Juqu Mengxun.

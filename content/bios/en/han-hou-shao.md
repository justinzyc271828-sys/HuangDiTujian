# The Later Young Emperor · Principal Deeds

Liu Hong, the Later Young Emperor, was enthroned after Empress Lü had the Former Young Emperor slain — nominally the fourth emperor of Han, inheriting the same regime of Lü regency: his title doubtful, real power in the empress dowager's hands, and the fate of [[han-qian-shao|his predecessor]] hanging over him.

In the seventh year of the regency, King You of Zhao was starved to death, and the accumulated hatred between the Lü clan and the Liu house broke into the open. In the eighth year, the empress dowager died, and the long-suppressed conflict exploded at once: Grand Commandant Zhou Bo took the Northern Army, and with Chancellor Chen Ping he destroyed the entire Lü clan; declaring that "the young emperor and the kings are no sons of Emperor Hui," they slew the Later Young Emperor and summoned the king of Dai, [[han-wen-di|Liu Heng]], to Chang'an.

In his four years the histories record almost nothing of the Later Young Emperor himself — his whole existence was the last seal of the Lü regency. With the Lü destroyed, that seal and both young emperors were wiped from the record together, and the Han succession returned to the Liu house. His tragedy and Emperor Wen's accession are two sides of one coin: the footnote to a regency's end, and the beginning of a new order.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Shiji*, "Basic Annals of Empress Lü," and the *Hanshu*, ch. 3 and ch. 4 (Benji of Empress Lü and of Emperor Wen); Tan Qixiang's atlas, vol. 2.

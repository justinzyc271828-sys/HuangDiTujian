# Emperor Taizu of Later Liang · Principal Deeds

Zhu Wen, a man of Dangshan in Songzhou, was a village rogue in his youth; when Huang Chao rose he joined the rebel host as a general, and in the 2nd year of Zhonghe surrendered to Tang, was renamed Quanzhong, and made military governor of Xuanwu — a surrendered rebel general who became the strongest warlord of the late Tang. For over twenty years he swallowed the warlords of the Central Plains, "holding the emperor to command the lords": in the 1st year of Tianyou he forced Zhaozong to Luoyang and slew him; and he drowned over thirty courtiers at the Baima relay — the slaughter of the clear stream.

In the 1st year of Kaiping Emperor Ai abdicated; Zhu Wen took the throne, founded Liang at Kaifeng, and changed the era — Tang fell, and the Five Dynasties began. Five years he reigned, "harsh and cruel in government," "lewd without limit," until in the 2nd year of Qianhua his son Zhu Yougui slew him, aged sixty-one.

The histories say: "a bandit who took the Tang throne; the histories record his cruelty." A surrendered rebel who took the Tang realm, his cruelty and lust were the worst of the Five Dynasties founders; but his "taking of Tang" was the inevitable end of the late-Tang warlordism. The chaos of the Five Dynasties begins with Zhu Wen — the age of Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms opens here.

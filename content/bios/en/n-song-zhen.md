# Zhao Heng, Emperor Zhenzong of Song · Major Deeds

Zhao Heng, third son of [[n-song-tai-zong|Emperor Taizong]], took the throne in 997 and changed the era to Xianping. His early years were served by chancellors Li Hang and Wang Dan; historians speak of a "Xianping golden age."

In 1004 Liao marched south in force. Chancellor Kou Zhun pressed the emperor to campaign in person; Zhenzong crossed the river to Chanzhou and concluded the Chanyuan Treaty: the two states became brothers, with Song paying an annual tribute of silver and silk. The treaty bought a century of peace, though the tribute system remained contested by later historians.

In his later years Zhenzong wearied of rule. In 1008 he fabricated a heavenly letter and performed the Fengshan sacrifices on Mount Tai; the lavish rites drained the treasury and governance slackened - summed up by historians as "Chanyuan settled, then the heavenly letter squandered."

He died in 1022; his son Zhao Zhen succeeded under the regency of Empress Liu, becoming [[n-song-ren|Emperor Renzong]].

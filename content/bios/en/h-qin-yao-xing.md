# Yao Xing, High Ancestor of Later Qin · Principal Deeds

Yao Xing, styled Zilue, was the eldest son of Yao Chang. On his father's death in the 8th year of Jianchu he took the throne at Chang'an. His twenty-two years were the height of Later Qin: in the early Huangchu years he broke Fu Deng, destroyed the remnants of Former Qin, and roughly united Guanzhong and Longxi — "the Xiongnu of the Longyou submitted" — and he took Luoyang and held Hedong. The realm stretched from Hexi in the west to Luoyang in the east, one of the strong states of the later Sixteen.

His rule favored letters and Buddhism: in the 3rd year of Hongshi he brought Kumarajiva to Chang'an, where the master translated the scriptures before throngs of monks — "all quarters looked up to him" — and Chang'an became the center of scripture translation; he founded a law academy, summoned hundreds of students from the provinces to study and examined their merits; in his leisure from government he lectured on the sutras himself. The culture of his court was unique among the Sixteen States.

But his succession was ill-managed: the heir Hong and his brother Bi contended, and while Xing lay ill his sons fought, and the state's strength ebbed. He died in the 1st year of Yonghe; in the 13th, Liu Yu of Eastern Jin destroyed Later Qin. The historians credit him with "care for government" and note "though law and punishment were imperfect, manners and teaching were admirable": in civil rule he stands first among the Sixteen States' sovereigns, but in arms he fell short of his father.

# Emperor Taizu of Later Zhou · Principal Deeds

Guo Wei, a man of Yaoshan in Xingzhou, styled Wenzhong, rose "from the ranks" and served Later Han by merit, reaching the posts of commissioner of military affairs and garrison commander of Yedu. In the 3rd year of Qianyou the Yin emperor slew his regents and sent secret orders against Guo Wei; Guo Wei marched south, the Yin emperor was slain, and Later Han was no more. Entering Kaifeng, he first set up the Han prince Liu Yun and then deposed him — in the 1st year of Guangshun (951) Guo Wei took the throne as the realm Zhou, with its capital at Kaifeng, changing the era to Guangshun.

Three years he reigned, bent on reform: he abolished the military farms, lightened the harsh taxes, and removed the cruel punishments, "lenient and loving to the people," ending the tribute-seeking and forbidding luxury — the foundations of Later Zhou were laid. In the 1st year of Xiande (954) he died at fifty-one; without a son, his adopted son Chai Rong (his nephew) succeeded — Emperor Shizong, the last and strongest sovereign of the Five Dynasties.

The histories say: "he rose from the ranks with the ambition of the realm." A soldier who took the throne and ruled with benevolence, the worthiest of the Five Dynasties founders; his adopted son's reforms and northern campaigns stood on his base. Zhou lasted three generations and ten years, yet "the government of Zhou was the best of the Five Dynasties" — and the Song, taking Zhou's place, inherited Zhou's legacy.

# Qifu Guoren, Ancestor Lie of Western Qin · Principal Deeds

Qifu Guoren was the chief of the Qifu Xianbei of Longxi. After the Fei River defeat, with Former Qin dissolving, he held Yuanchuan in the 1st year of Jianyi and styled himself Great Chanyu — the beginning of Western Qin. In his four years he broke the neighboring Xianbei tribes, the Pilan and the Migu and the rest, extending his lands to Jincheng; he dealt with the powers of Guanzhong and Longxi, accepting from Fu Deng the titles of King of Yuanchuan and more.

He died in the 4th year of Jianyi, his brother Gangui carrying on Western Qin. Under Gangui and then Qifu Chipan the state had some measure of size, but it was ever cramped in one corner of Longxi, fighting Later Qin, Southern Liang, and Northern Liang, and was finally destroyed by the Xia state.

The historians judge him: a rough founding, and the realm's years were few. Western Qin was among the weakest of the Sixteen States; it rose on Former Qin's ruin and fell before the stronger powers — the difficulty of a single Xianbei tribe holding its own through decades of chaos. Guoren's name is but a moment in the storm.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 125 (records of Qifu Guoren), ch. 125 (records of Qifu Gangui).

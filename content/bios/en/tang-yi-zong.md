# Emperor Yizong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Cui, first named Wen, was the eldest son of Xuanzong. On his father's death in the 13th year of Dazhong he took the throne, changing the era to Xiantong. Yizong was "extravagant without limit": he loved music and pleasure, "three thousand of the inner palace serving by turns"; and he doted on Buddhism — in the 14th year of Xiantong he welcomed the Famen Temple relic, "the imperial guards leading, drums and music stretching for tens of li," spending tens of millions — the relic that Han Yu had warned against, welcomed with more fervor than ever.

His reign saw the risings begin: in the 9th year of Xiantong the Guilin garrison rose under Pang Xun and marched north to Xuzhou, taking prefectures and counties, "shaking the Tang house" — suppressed in the end, but "though Pang Xun fell, the disaster of Huang Chao was already seeded." He left the government to the chancellors Lu Yan and Wei Baohang, "the government coming from many mouths, bribes running openly."

He died in the 14th year of Xiantong, aged forty-one. The histories say: "extravagant and Buddhist-mad, he let the bandits flame up." His reign was the prologue to the great disorders of the Tang's end: Pang Xun opened the way, and Wang Xianzhi and Huang Chao followed — the fall of Tang began with Yizong's extravagance and misrule.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 19a ("Benji of Emperor Yizong").

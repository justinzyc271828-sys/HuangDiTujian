# Yang Pu, Emperor Rui of Wu · Principal Deeds

Yang Pu, fourth son of Yang Xingmi, took the throne in the 2nd year of Wuyi (920) after his brother Longyan's death — by then the government of Yang Wu lay wholly with Xu Wen and his adopted son Xu Zhigao. In the 1st year of Qianzhen (927), moving toward the abdication, Zhigao first had Pu take the imperial title; the Xu design was plain.

Seventeen years he reigned as the Xu's puppet: after Xu Wen's death, Xu Zhigao (Li Bian) governed, was made Prince of Qi, and given the nine gifts; in the 3rd year of Tianzuo (937) Zhigao forced the abdication, founding the realm Qi (soon renamed Tang) — the Yang Wu was no more, four rulers and thirty-six years from Xingmi's kingship. Pu, settled at Runzhou, died within months.

The histories say of Pu: "he ceded the realm with folded hands." Yang Wu fell not to Pu's dullness but to the Xu's strength — Xu Wen, an adopted son, took the Yang power, and the "taking of Wu" was already fixed from Xingmi's death. Pu, a puppet sovereign, only folded his hands; the rise of Southern Tang was the continuation of Yang Wu.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Xin Wudaishi*, ch. 61 ("Annals of Wu").

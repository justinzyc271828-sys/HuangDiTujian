# Emperor Suzong of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Heng, third son of Xuanzong. In the 1st year of Zhide (756), in the An–Shi rebellion, Xuanzong fled to Shu; the heir Heng, parting from Xuanzong at Mawei, went north and took the throne at Lingwu, changing the era to Zhide and honoring Xuanzong as Grand Emperor from afar — the restoration of the Tang began at Lingwu. Suzong's first task was the rebellion: in the 2nd year of Zhide Guo Ziyi, Li Guangbi, and the others, with the Uyghur cavalry, recovered the two capitals; but "borrowing the Uyghurs left a lasting plague," and the Uyghurs' arrogance was a disaster to come.

In the 1st year of Qianyuan he sent nine commissioners to besiege An Qingxu at Ye; without a supreme commander, the host was routed at Xiangzhou, and Shi Siming rose again, slew Qingxu, and the rebellion flared anew. In the Shangyuan and Baoying years Suzong lay ill while Empress Zhang and Li Fuguo contended — the palace disorders unended; he died in the Changsheng Hall in the 1st year of Baoying, aged fifty-two.

The histories say: "a hard-won throne: the two capitals recovered, but Hebei unquiet." Suzong, a heir enthroned in the storm, restored the Tang house; but his use of Li Fuguo and Empress Zhang opened the age of eunuch power, and the feudatory division of Hebei was fixed in his reign — the rebellion ended, but the decline of Tang could not be reversed.

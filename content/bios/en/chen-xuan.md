# Emperor Xuan of Chen · Principal Deeds

Chen Xu, styled Shaoshi, was the second son of the Prince Zhaolie of Shixing and younger brother of Emperor Wen. In the 2nd year of Guangda he deposed his nephew Bozong and took the throne, changing the era to Taijian. His fourteen years were the height of Chen's arms: in the 5th year of Taijian he sent Wu Mingche and the others north, broke Northern Qi, and recovered all the Huainan — "the widest borders of Chen"; he sent envoys to Northern Zhou in friendship, and the southern power stood up again for a moment.

In the 9th year of Taijian Northern Zhou destroyed Northern Qi, and Chen contended for Xu and Yan; in the 10th year Wu Mingche attacked Pengcheng and was shattered at Lüliang, taken by Zhou — the Lüliang disaster cost Chen its finest troops, and the Huainan was lost again. Xuan's "aim was Henan, the enterprise failed at the last step": had he fixed his strategy when Qi was dying, he might have contended for the Central Plains; but Chen's strength was spent, and it never marched north again.

He died in the 14th year of Taijian at fifty-three; his son Shubao, the Last Sovereign, succeeded. The histories say: "of great strategy, but of narrow vision." The rise of Chen was Xuan's northern expedition; the fall of Chen was the Lüliang disaster — his game against Qi and Zhou could not match the strength of the north. Eight years after his death, Sui destroyed Chen, and the Southern Dynasties were at an end.

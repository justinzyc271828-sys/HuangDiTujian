# Emperor Wen of Song · Principal Deeds

Liu Yilong, styled Che'er, was the third son of Emperor Wu. After the Young Emperor's deposition he came from Jiangling to Jiankang, took the throne at eighteen, and changed the era to Yuanjia. In the 3rd year of Yuanjia he slew Xu Xianzhi and Fu Liang and marched against Xie Hui at Jiangling — ruling in person, and the Yuanjia recovery began. Thirty years he encouraged farming and learning, lightened the laws and the levies, and the households multiplied; "grain left standing in the fields" — the one era of modest plenty in the southern dynasties. In the 16th year he founded the four academies of Metaphysics, History, Letters, and Confucianism, and culture shone.

His reign had two great errors. The first was the northern expeditions: in the 7th year Dao Yanzhi's campaign recovered Henan, lost again that winter; in the 27th year the great expedition failed — Wang Xuanmo beaten, Emperor Taiwu of Wei crossing the Huai and riding to Guabu, the lands north of the river laid waste. "Yuanjia, in haste, sealed the Wolf's Head and gained a panic flight north" (Xin Qiji) — the south never recovered its strength. The second was the succession: he long refused to fix the succession, destroyed his brother the Prince of Pengcheng, then trusted the heir Liu Shao — until, in the second month of the 30th year of Yuanjia, Shao slew him in the He Hall, aged forty-seven.

"Sharp and benevolent, his Yuanjia years well governed" — yet the expeditions too hasty and kin slaying kin, he ended in catastrophe. Of the southern sovereigns Wen was among the best; but the substance of the Yuanjia recovery was spent on the two expeditions and the family slaughter — the decline of Liu Song began in his own reign.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Songshu*, ch. 5 ("Benji of Emperor Wen"), ch. 99 (the Two Parricides).

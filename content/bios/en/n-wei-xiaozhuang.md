# Emperor Xiaozhuang of Northern Wei · Principal Deeds

Yuan Ziyou, third son of the Prince of Pengcheng, Yuan Xie, was set up by Erzhu Rong in the 1st year of Jianyi, when Rong, using Empress Hu's poisoning of Xiaoming as his cause, marched south. His accession opened with the Heyin massacre: Rong drowned the empress and the infant emperor in the river and slew over two thousand princes and ministers — the elite of the Wei house, gone in a day. Ziyou sat as Rong's puppet.

In the 2nd year of Yong'an, Liang propped up the Wei prince Yuan Hao to enter Luoyang; the emperor fled north and soon returned with Rong. In the 3rd year of Yong'an, Rong's arrogance grew to usurpation, and Ziyou, plotting with his attendants, cut Rong down with his own hand in the Mingguang Hall — "the emperor slew Rong with his own hand, and Rong died" — a moment of satisfaction. But the Erzhu party was not spent: Erzhu Zhao, hearing of it, marched into Luoyang, seized Ziyou, and had him strangled at the Jinyang temple of three stories, aged twenty-four.

His will to avenge and his own hand against the strong minister were brave; but "the strength did not match," the Erzhu power was untouched, and the emperor died and the realm fell into chaos. The histories say he "had the bearing of a hero, but the age was against him": the slaying of Erzhu Rong was the one great deed of Wei's last years — yet only a dying ember, as the realm passed to the Erzhu, to Gao Huan, to Yuwen Tai.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Weishu*, ch. 10 ("Benji of Emperor Xiaozhuang"), ch. 74 (biography of Erzhu Rong).

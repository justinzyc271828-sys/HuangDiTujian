# Wang Yan, The Last Sovereign of Former Shu · Principal Deeds

Wang Yan, youngest son of Wang Jian, took the throne in the 1st year of Guangtian (918). He was "a child at ease": he loved his little verses, feasting without limit with Han Zhao, Pan Kang, and the other companions, "raising the Yishen Pavilion to drink with them"; he went abroad in secret dress, careless of government — "the government of Former Shu decayed from that day."

In the 1st year of Xiankang (925) Emperor Zhuangzong of Later Tang sent Guo Chongtao and the Prince of Wei to invade; the Tang army entered Shu, Wang Yan surrendered, and Former Shu was no more — eighteen years from Wang Jian's title, two rulers. Sent toward Luoyang, he was slain at the Qinchuan post station in mid-Tongguang (some say by decree), aged twenty-eight.

The histories say: "a child who lost the realm." Former Shu held the heavenly prefecture, with the means to stand; but Wang Yan "lost his realm to ease," and a decade's enterprise was spent in a day. Zhuangzong, who "cased the heads of the Liang lords," destroyed Shu — and then lost himself to the same ease: "the fall of Wang Yan's realm and the fall of Zhuangzong's self, the same two words: ease and pleasure."

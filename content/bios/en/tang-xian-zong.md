# Emperor Xianzong of Tang · Key Events

Li Chun reigned for fifteen years (805–820) under the era name Yuanhe and was the most revitalizing emperor of the Tang after the An Lushan Rebellion. This page builds its chronology on the "Annals of Xianzong" in the Old Book of Tang, checked against the "Annals of Xianzong" in the New Book of Tang and the Zizhi Tongjian (all held in the local reference library). The main line of his life comes down to two words: reducing the provinces.

Yuanhe 1 (806) showed his opening hand: first he suppressed Yang Huilin of Xia-Sui, then Liu Pi of Xichuan. Two victories in a row announced to every military province that the court would no longer be lenient. But the campaign against Wang Chengzong of Chengde in Yuanhe 4 (809) achieved limited results, and it taught him how deeply entrenched the Heshuo provinces were and how hard they would be to crack.

The real test was Huaixi. In Yuanhe 10 (815), Li Shidao of Pinglu sent assassins who killed the pro-war chancellor Wu Yuanheng in the streets of Chang'an, shocking court and country, yet the court's will to use force did not waver. In Yuanhe 12 (817) the chancellor Pei Du went in person to Huaixi to reassure and direct the armies, and the renowned general Li Su made a surprise entry into Caizhou on a snowy night and captured Wu Yuanji alive: the climax of Yuanhe province reduction and the most celebrated surprise attack of the mid-Tang. Around the following year (818), powerful Heshuo provinces such as Chengde for a time obeyed the court, in what the histories call the "Yuanhe Restoration."

In his later years he received the Buddha's relic from Famen Temple into Chang'an; Han Yu submitted a memorial of utmost remonstrance and was banished, leaving another face of ruler-minister relations and religious policy. In Yuanhe 15 (820) he died suddenly in Chang'an, an event the histories record as wrapped in suspicions involving eunuchs. After Emperor Muzong of Tang took the throne, the gains of province reduction quickly receded and Heshuo rebelled again. From the Yongzhen abdication of Shunzong at the opening to the sudden death at the close, whether the "Yuanhe Restoration" could have lasted is the question most worth pausing over in Xianzong's reign; read against the Zhenguan model of [[tang-tai-zong|Emperor Taizong of Tang]], it also shows the enormous gap in the empire's circumstances between the High Tang and the mid-Tang.

# Feng Ba, Emperor Wencheng of Northern Yan · Principal Deeds

Feng Ba was a Han of Xindu in Changle. In the last years of Later Yan the emperor Murong Xi was a tyrant; in the 1st year of Taiping Ba and Gao Yun and the others slew Xi and set up Yun; when Yun was slain by Li Ban and the rest, Ba took the heavenly throne himself, with his capital at Longcheng — the beginning of Northern Yan, the one Han-founded state of the Sixteen.

His rule was lenient and pacific: he lightened corvée and taxes, encouraged farming and mulberries, "and the people praised it"; he gathered the refugees and made the levies easy; he married peace with the Rouran, steadying the northern border; he founded schools and honored Confucian learning. Cramped in Liaoxi though it was, Northern Yan's government was among the most admirable of the later Sixteen States. In the 22nd year of Taiping, as Ba lay ill, his brother Feng Hong slew the heir Feng Yi and took the throne; pressed by Northern Wei, Hong fled to Goguryeo, and Northern Yan was no more.

The historians say of Ba: "benevolent and generous, careful in economy," yet "he held his borders and cared for his people, but could not press into the Central Plains." A Han regime ringed by barbarian states, Northern Yan held on over twenty years by its lenient rule and its marriages of peace; and with its fall, the Sixteen States age itself drew to its end.

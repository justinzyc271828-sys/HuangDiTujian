# Emperor Hui of Jin · Principal Deeds

Sima Zhong, styled Zhengdu, was the second son of Emperor Wu, and his dullness was known to all: in the Huayuan park, hearing frogs croak, he asked whether they croaked "for office or for private gain"; in the great famine, told the people were starving, he asked, "Why do they not eat minced meat?" Emperor Wu himself doubted him, but Empress Yang and Jia Chong's party pressed for his appointment as heir — planting the ruin of Western Jin.

In the 1st year of Taixi Wu died and Zhong took the throne at thirty-two. Empress Jia ruled: in the 1st year of Yuankang she slew Yang Jun, then the Prince of Runan and Wei Guan, governing at will; in the 9th year of Yuankang she had the heir Sima Yu falsely charged and slain, and the Prince of Zhao rose against her — the War of the Eight Princes broke out. For ten years the princes deposed and restored him: Lun usurped, the Prince of Qi restored, the Prince of Changsha slew the Prince of Qi, the Princes of Hejian and Chengdu fought, Zhang Fang carried the emperor to Chang'an, and the Prince of Donghai brought him back to Luoyang, where he died in the 1st year of Guangxi — poisoned by Yue, some say.

Sixteen years on the throne, he never knew he was in a storm, carried about by this prince and that. The war exhausted Jin's strength; Li Xiong took Shu and Liu Yuan styled himself king of Han — the Five Barbarian invasions had begun. The historians do not blame Zhong himself; they blame Wu's choice of heir. "Why not eat minced meat?" passed into the language as the eternal byword of a fool, and of a dynasty that entrusted its realm to one.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jinshu*, ch. 4 ("Benji of Emperor Hui"), ch. 31 (biographies of the empresses).

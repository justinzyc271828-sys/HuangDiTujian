# Emperor Shang of Tang · Principal Deeds

Li Chongmao, fourth son of Zhongzong, was enfeoffed Prince of Wen. In the sixth month of the 4th year of Jinglong, Zhongzong died suddenly; Empress Wei took the regency and set up Chongmao, changing the era to Tanglong — sixteen years old, the puppet of Wei.

That same day in the sixth month, the Prince of Linzi, Li Longji (Xuanzong), with the Princess Taiping and the others, staged the Tanglong coup and slew Empress Wei and her party; Ruizong was brought in, Chongmao yielded the throne to his uncle, and was reduced to Prince of Wen. Later moved to Fangzhou, he died around the 2nd year of Kaiyuan, about twenty — the histories call him Emperor Shang or the Young Emperor, and the *Xin Tangshu* gives him a benji.

Less than a month on the throne; the histories say: "a child puppet caught in the palace coup." The palace coups of the Tang came thickest in the Zhongzong–Shang years: the Shenlong coup, the Jinglong disorders, the Tanglong coup — three changes within a decade. Shang was but a passing figure; the gazetteer keeps him in the Tang line as quasi-emperor.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Tangshu*, ch. 7 ("Benji of Emperor Zhongzong"), and the *Xin Tangshu*, ch. 4 ("Benji of Emperor Zhongzong").

# Emperor Wu of Liang · Key Events

Xiao Yan was the founding ruler of the Liang dynasty. In Zhongxing 1 (501) he marched east from Xiangyang, stormed Jiankang, and seized control of Southern Qi's endgame; the next year he accepted Qi's abdication and took the throne, founding Liang with the capital still at Jiankang. He would reign for nearly half a century—one of the longest reigns in this atlas.

At the founding he appointed Erudites of the Five Classics and revived letters and learning; the long stability from Tianjian to Datong brought literature, Buddhist learning, and ritual music to a simultaneous flowering, counted among the cultural peaks of the Southern Dynasties. In foreign ventures there was the Fushan Weir project of Tianjian 13 (514), damming the Huai River to attack Wei—a vast and deeply controversial work.

His other face was his devotion to Buddhism. From Datong 1 (527) the rites at Tongtai Temple escalated steadily; he repeatedly surrendered himself to the temple and was ransomed by his officials at great price, binding Buddhist ceremony to the state treasury.

The late-reign turning point came in Taiqing 1 (547): Hou Jing came offering his allegiance, and though the debate over accepting him was fierce, Xiao Yan finally decided to take him in. The next year the Hou Jing Rebellion erupted, disaster struck Jiankang, and Taicheng was besieged; in Taiqing 3 (549) Taicheng fell, and Emperor Wu, held under Hou Jing's control, died of grief, hunger, and illness. Emperor Jianwen succeeded him while Hou Jing still held Jiankang. The able ruler of the first half, the man trapped by Buddha and Hou Jing in the second—these are the two faces of the verdict on his reign.

Read alongside [[n-wei-xiaowen|Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei]]: one north, one south; one recast Northern Wei through sinicizing reform, the other sustained a Southern Dynasties cultural peak through letters and Buddhist devotion. And the siege of Taicheng wrote the footnote to the Southern Dynasties' turn from height to decline.

# Liu Jun, Emperor Ruizong of Northern Han · Principal Deeds

Liu Jun, originally Liu Chengjun, was the second son of Liu Chong. After Chong's death following the Gaoping defeat in the 7th year of Qianyou (954), Jun took the throne. With the elite of Northern Han spent at Gaoping, Jun "served Liao ever more humbly": yearly tribute to the Khitan for support; and, "holding himself a scion of the Liu house," he defied Later Zhou and Song.

After the Song founder took the throne, the Song armies attacked Taiyuan again and again: in the Kaibao years they besieged it several times, and Northern Han, leaning on Liao's aid, held — "the city of Taiyuan, the strongest under heaven." Jun died in the 12th year of Tianhui (968), around forty-three; his adopted sons Ji'en and Jiyuan followed, and the realm lingered until the 4th year of Taiping Xingguo (979), when Taizong of Song campaigned in person, the Liao relief failed, and Northern Han surrendered — the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms were at an end.

The histories say: "he could hold, but not extend." Northern Han defied Zhou and Song for over thirty years on the sliver of Hedong, by the strength of Taiyuan and the Liao alliance; but "Northern Han lived on the standoff of Song and Liao" — and with its fall the Song–Liao struggle began in earnest. Jun's stewardship, though without glory, was the stubborn tail of the Five Dynasties.

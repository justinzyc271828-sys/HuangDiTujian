# Tufa Wugu, Martial King of Southern Liang · Principal Deeds

Tufa Wugu was the chief of the Tufa Xianbei of Hexi. In the 1st year of Taichu he turned against Lü Guang of Later Liang, styled himself King of Xiping, and built the fort of Lianchuan as his capital, holding the Huangzhong — the beginning of Southern Liang. "Bold and great of purpose," he extended his lands to Guangwu and Huanghe in the 2nd year and took the title King of Wuwei; he drew in the Han gentry, Shi Song and the rest, and planned to hold Hexi.

In the 3rd year of Taichu, drunk, he fell from his horse, hurt his ribs, and died, around forty. His brothers Lilugu and then Nutan succeeded, and Southern Liang rose and fell by turns — Lilugu moving the capital to Xiping, Nutan to Ledu, fighting Later Qin, Northern Liang, and Western Qin. Cramped and poor in men, it was finally destroyed by Western Qin.

The historians judge Wugu: "a rough founding of Southern Liang, and the realm's years were few." A Hexi Xianbei tribe seizing its chance on Later Liang's decline, it rose swiftly; his death by drink left the succession weak. The records' preface notes the legend that the Tufa and the Tuoba share a common origin — the fate of the one house rising while the other fell is a mirror of the age.

# The Last Sovereign of Later Liang · Principal Deeds

Zhu Youzhen, third son of the Liang Taizu. In the 2nd year of Qianhua Taizu was slain by Yougui, who took the throne; in the 1st year of Fengli the Prince of Jun, Youzhen, plotting with Zhao Yan and the rest, drew in the imperial guards, slew Yougui, and took the throne, restoring the era Qianhua. Ten years he reigned — among the longest of the Five Dynasties; but "weak and timid, no hand for war," he contended year after year with the King of Jin, Li Cunxu, for Hebei — at Baima, Yangliu, and the rest the Liang armies were beaten again and again, and Liang's strength ebbed.

In the Zhenming and Longde years "the government came from many mouths," Duan Ning and Zhao Yan in power; in the 3rd year of Longde Li Cunxu took the throne at Weizhou (Later Tang) and rode at once against Kaifeng. Learning the Tang army was at hand, the Last Sovereign climbed the Jianguo Tower, had Huangfu Lin kill him — "weeping, he received the blade" — and Later Liang was no more, aged thirty-six.

The histories say: "he could not control his generals, and fell to the Shatuo at last." From Taizu's abdication to the Last Sovereign's fall, two emperors and seventeen years — one of the briefest of the Five Dynasties. Liang fell to Taizu's tyranny and the Last Sovereign's weakness; and the rise of Li Cunxu opened the three Shatuo dynasties — Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han.

> References: the punctuated critical edition of the *Jiu Wudaishi*, ch. 8–10 ("Annals of the Liang Last Sovereign").
